# 04 - Data layers: infrastructure, cameras, imagery, weather and event scene data (behavioural reverse-engineering)

Purpose: this note records, layer by layer, what the studied browser globe application does for its infrastructure layers (submarine cables, data centres, dams, mapped installations, plate-reader camera locations), its camera and imagery layers (public camera network, recent satellite imagery, street-level photos), its utility layers (radio with a local software-defined-radio card, directions), its weather layers (wind, rain radar, satellite clouds, lightning, cyclones and the shared weather timeline), the bundled Nepal flood scene data and the regional extras that sit beside the layers. For each: data provider, refresh cadence, caps and limits, viewport behaviour, marker colouring, click/metadata card fields, filters and controls, fallbacks, empty and error states, and key or credential needs. It is written so a clean-room team can rebuild an original product for Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah (Arabic + English, right-to-left) without reading the original source. Numbers are the values observed in the studied product and are sizing hints, not requirements. Confidence tags: [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE], [FROM-TESTS], [FROM-DOCS-ONLY], [INFERRED]; untagged statements are observed in source. Anything not confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED. Pass 3 merged the feature census: mechanism detail for ground layers is in the ground-level census (13d) and for weather, hazards and infrastructure in the weather census (13e); section pointers sit inside each section below, and section 16 summarises the street-traffic mechanism and points to the hazard layers documented in the movement note. Shared conventions (health words, honesty rules, declutter, selection and tracking) are defined in the companion note on movement and event layers and are only summarised here.

## 0. Conventions used by the layers in this note

- **Layer grouping.** Cameras group: Cameras, Recent Imagery, Street Level. Infrastructure group: Mapped ALPR Cameras, Mapped Installations, Data Centers, Submarine Cables, Dams (this order). Weather group: Wind, Rain radar, Satellite clouds, Lightning density, Cyclone advisories. Utilities group: Directions, Radio. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- **Nothing in these groups switches itself on at a fresh visit.** A first-run mission launcher offers explicit choices; it deliberately does not auto-enable live feeds. A layer is turned on by a user action, a share link, the voice agent or an automation tool. Enablement set by the user, voice or tools is remembered in the browser between sessions. An "Infrastructure" mission tile (data centres + dams + cables in one click, about 5,700 entities) was built and cut because it hurt frame rate at globe zoom; the lesson is to declutter before offering one-click bundles. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE / FROM-DOCS-ONLY]
- **Health vocabulary.** ON, LOADING, DEGRADED, STALE, PARTIAL, FALLBACK, UNAVAILABLE on the row; guidance statuses ("zoom in", "run a search") read as normal ON. A bundled dataset that fails to load is treated as a broken install: row reads UNAVAILABLE (never a green ON over an empty globe), and the next enable retries the load. A layer that needs a missing key shows KEY REQUIRED and its tooltip names which key to add.
- **Stems.** Bundled point-like infrastructure (data centres, dams, cable references) is shown as a ground anchor with a thin vertical "stem" and a label. The stem tip height is recomputed from camera distance so every stem stays about 65 px tall on screen (66 px for cables) whatever the zoom. Stems are re-measured only when the camera settles; a slow motion probe (every 2 s, only for camera moves over about 250 m) covers tracked or orbiting cameras that never "settle". A stem tip change under 0.5 m is ignored.
- **Globe levels of detail for bundled sets (data centres, dams).** Active stems are budgeted by camera height: at 3,000 km and above 80 stems, from 200 km to 3,000 km 200 stems, below 200 km up to 420 stems. Ranking is label priority (named features first) blended with a bounded distance penalty, plus an incumbency bonus (smaller than the "has a name" gap, so an unnamed stem never outlives a fresh named one); eviction waits 2 passes or 4 s. Opposite of the camera layer: zooming out must show fewer items. Cables use their own separate renderer and budget.
- **Ambient cards for bundled infrastructure.** Text cards are painted by a shared overlay: not clickable, visible out to 14,000 km, fade beginning at 250 km, scale 1.0 at 250 km shrinking to 0.62 at 9,000 km, a 15 px gap above the anchor, keyhole edge fade, horizon culling, not occluded by terrain. Each source keeps a cohort of at most 160 and 96 collision slots; the shared lane tops out at 192 cards.
- **Credits.** Every layer registers its attribution into one expandable credits panel, visible in clean-view and recording mode.
- **Voice names.** The voice agent knows these layers by plain names (for example "undersea cables", "bases", "webcams", "street level", "latest imagery", "driving directions", "hurricanes"). Analyst and automation tools can query cables (id and name only, by area or name), data centres and dams (name, operator, capacity or river and output, position; default 25 rows), and camera snapshots.
- **Persistence and sharing.** Layer options (wind model, field and units, weather opacity and satellite product and treatment, camera coverage mode / projection / auto hop, recent-imagery box, pins, mode, street-level provider switches and filter) are carried in share links and in remembered layer state. Timeline history and the swipe split are share-only or never stored. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]

## 6. CCTV / public camera network

### 6.1 Catalogue and providers
- About 3,900 public cameras in the studied build (DOC claim). The default per-pack caps add up to more than the catalogue ceiling, so on a good day the shared ceiling trims; the actual count depends on live availability [INFERRED]. Cameras are assembled from per-agency "packs", each capped and nearest-first to its own anchor cities, sharing a catalogue-wide ceiling of 4,000 (hard bound 5,000, floor 8) divided round-robin so a low global cap thins every region instead of starving the last pack. A duplicate id resolves last-pack-wins so operator overrides replace live records.
- Packs and defaults: Austin (open data, only cameras whose status is turned on, 250, ranked by distance to downtown; heading read from the record when present), Texas DOT (Austin and San Antonio districts by default, 25 districts exist, cap 500; only devices online; heading only from an explicit travel token such as EB/WB in the name; frames arrive as base64 JSON the server decodes after verifying the official origin and JPEG header), Caltrans California (districts 4/7/11/3, 300; heading from the direction field), TfL London JamCams (250, attribution required, no heading data), Ontario 511 (up to 1,000, six anchor cities; heading from direction or view description), Fintraffic Finland (300 of about 2,275 presets from 809 stations, one preset = one camera; only collecting stations; no heading in the data so each preset takes a hash-derived bearing that fans presets apart), DriveBC (250 nearest Vancouver and Victoria; compass orientation codes give heading; partner credits such as city or transit cameras carried per camera), Estonia (Tallinn intersections 255 from a curated file with curated headings; Tarktee road-weather cameras 179 from a DATEX feed), Live Traffic NSW (250; image host needs a browser identity; heading from direction), Open Calgary (220; no heading derived because the fields that look like one are unreliable), Norway Statens vegvesen (default 900, about 850 working; faulty cameras dropped; about 135 also publish live HLS video; no heading), Delaware DelDOT (300; active cameras only; live HLS video; heading from NB/SB/EB/WB tokens in the title for about one in five), Warendorf (one municipal webcam from a curated file), New York DOT (250, **off unless the operator opts in**, needs the operator's own data-sharing agreement). All packs except Austin and Caltrans have a kill switch; Austin and Caltrans are always on. Operators can also add their own cameras by file or inline list; a hand-authored entry may be marked as a curated pose. A pilot Tokyo sample file ships with demo video clips.
- If no live source is reachable the client falls back to a built-in set of 18 seed cameras over New York, San Francisco, Tokyo, London, Paris, Washington DC, Dubai (DIFC Loop, Downtown East) and Austin, with hand-set poses.
- Each camera record: id, city, name, position, feed type (still or video), provider label, optional credit, short code, heading, heading confidence, FOV, range, pitch, mount height, ground elevation, source status/message, calibration state. Precomputed ground heights for about 3,445 cameras ship with the app so placement needs no sampling.
- **Pose defaults.** Live packs publish a fabricated "raw prior": when the feed gives a heading, pitch -24, FOV 56, range 210 m, mount 10 m (12 for Texas); when it does not, heading = hash of the id with "low" confidence, pitch -18, FOV 44, range 145 m, mount 8 m. Only when a pack gives nothing at all does the client fall back to FOV 74, range 700 m, pitch -17, mount 24 m. Client clamps: FOV 20 to 125, range 120 to 2,200 m, pitch -55 to -2, mount 6 to 120 m (the pose model itself allows FOV to 130, range to 5,000, pitch -70 to 10, mount 2 to 240). (This corrects the earlier note that said 70 to 74 degrees / 700 m / -17 for live cameras.)
- Catalogue cached on the server 15 minutes; each pack fetch times out at 15 s and a failing pack contributes nothing (a fully empty refresh keeps serving the previous catalogue); frames fetched at request time and never stored (8 s deadline, 16 MB body cap); clients send camera ids, never URLs; redirects refused. Frame fallback chain: upstream picture, then a Google Street View still when a server key exists (960 x 540, outdoor only), then a generated placeholder card ("CCTV FEED PLACEHOLDER" with label, city, id, status and timestamp on a hash-tinted scanline background). Live video goes through a bounded in-memory HLS relay: 2 sessions at once, 8 consumer leases each, 15 s idle lease expiry, 12 segments / 24 MiB / 4 MiB per segment, playlists up to 256 KiB, 2 s playlist polling, 12 s to become ready, no disk, no RTMP, no encrypted/fMP4/byte-range playlists. Health entries are kept per camera (bounded to the catalogue ceiling).
- Content stance: frames are relayed unaltered; no face, plate or object recognition; no person search. A voice question about the scene may attach a downscaled screenshot to the AI request.

### 6.2 Layer behaviour
| Aspect | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Enabling | The row shows loading progress as a "loading frames n/total" chip (split-flap text), then a "camera grid ready" flash that hides after 1.5 s. Geometry is computed in small batches nearest-first: 4 records per 120 ms normally, 2 per 250 ms while tracking or cockpit owns the view, progress notified about every 300 ms or 10 batches. Switching CCTV on from the panel button, or any of NEAREST / PREV / NEXT / dropdown / FOCUS (each also turns the layer on first), activates the nearest camera and flies to it (1.6 s) when none is active, unless an aircraft is tracked or the cockpit owns the camera (then it only activates). Camera geometry waits up to 8 s for ground priors, then proceeds on catalogue values and corrects late. |
| Icons | 24 px camera icons stay visible at every zoom and always on top (far-side icons hidden by horizon culling): idle cyan #6be8ff (88% alpha), active amber #ffd97a; scale 1.25 at 350 m falling to 0.42 at 4,000 km. |
| Cadences | Active camera frame refresh 10 s; idle 60 s; health sync every 7 s; projection refresh 10 s active / 60 s idle; texture swap at most 1 s; frame content change detected by a 64 x 36 signature; placeholder repaint throttled to 750 ms. Upstream or Street View attempts abort after 8 s; only one same-camera image request in flight; a failed refresh keeps the last good picture. |
| Camera panel | Starts collapsed; preview frame (live video canvas for HLS cameras, otherwise still), a source badge, a one-line meta, controls, calibration block, typed scene summary. It auto-expands when the active camera changes to a new camera (not on every timed hop). |
| Source badge | "KIND . STATUS" (for example a still source that is OK), "FRAME . LOADING", "FRAME . UNAVAILABLE", or "SOURCE . UNKNOWN" when off. When the upstream has no frame the panel shows a Street View still or the placeholder card and the badge names which. |
| Meta line | City . HDG deg (tagged "(ESTIMATED)" for synthetic bearings) . FOV . RANGE m . MONITOR or OFF (projection) . calibration badge . provider/source label . optional partner credit . source message. Before any camera is active: "N cameras loaded - click a camera to activate" (or "enable CCTV to activate"). With no catalogue: "Enable CCTV to load camera intersections". |
| Controls | CCTV on/off; NEAREST; PREV / NEXT; a camera dropdown ("city . name", picking flies 2.2 s); FOCUS; COVERAGE (three states COVERAGE OFF, COVERAGE ON, VIEWSHED ON, default ON); AUTO HOP OFF/ON; PROJECTION ON/OFF (default ON); CAL chip; ADJUST; SAVE CAL; RESET CAL; clickable pose readout. Clicking an icon or its painted card activates the camera; clicking empty globe deselects the active camera. |
| Auto hop | **Default OFF; default interval 18 s** (resolved). The interval (clamped 8 to 90 s) can only be changed through voice or tool control; the panel has only the on/off button. Needs at least 2 cameras. At each interval it advances to the next camera in catalogue order; if the viewer has moved into a new view band since the last hop it snaps to the nearest camera instead. An explicit empty-space deselect suspends the timer (the setting stays on) until the user next activates a camera or toggles hop on. |
| FOCUS | Flies to frame the camera: offset along its heading, pitch -22 degrees, range max(280 m, 1.18 x camera range) around a sphere of max(40 m, 0.36 x range), 2.2 s (1.8 s from the panel). Refused while an aircraft is tracked ("tracking holds the view") or the cockpit is active. |
| Coverage | ON draws a pyramid wireframe (four corner rays plus the far-cap rectangle) per camera, created lazily: the active camera always, plus the nearest 14 within 1.8 km of it; idle wireframes cyan 24% alpha, active green #8dff87 edges and #d7ff8d centre lines (dimmer where hidden behind buildings so they read through them); low-confidence bearings draw dashed. VIEWSHED fills each camera's frustum as a translucent colour-coded volume (golden-angle hue per camera) welded to the same five points; no extra scene queries. |
| Projection | The live frame is painted on a monitor-like plane at the far cap of the frustum, on a 1920 x 1080 canvas, so the picture appears inside the 3D city "like a hologram". An activation-time obstruction probe clamps the plane to 4 m short of the first thing the frustum axis hits (floor 12 m; the range slider overrides the clamp), the plane's footprint is lifted rigidly (by at most 60 m beyond what the mount ground needs) so a 3 x 3 grid of support points clears the ground by 2 m (see section 6.3), and the cap-centre never sinks below 2 m above ground so a steep pitch cannot bury it. Plane outline cyan #6be8ff at 55%. Texture swaps only when the new canvas has content (no white flashes). The projection loop stops itself when idle. |
| Ambient thumbnail cards | Small screen-space cards with paced still frames for nearby stills (96 x 54 px thumbnails from 192 x 108 frames; at least 112 px apart; kept clear of the top 18% of the screen, max 150 px): budget 20 / 28 / 40 cards by zoom (16 while geometry is draining), ranked by a 50/50 blend of eye distance and screen-centre offset, distributed over a 5 x 4 screen grid first, incumbency factor 0.8 (a challenger must be over 20% nearer), eviction grace 2 passes or 5 s; reselected only on camera settle; scale full out to 1.8 km, 0.45 at 6 km, fading out between 7.5 and 9.5 km; one frame fetch per second layer-wide (bursts of up to 4 at 250 ms on cold fill); 96 frames cached; failed cards retry from 15 s backing off to 5 min; per-source cadences Austin 5 min, TfL and Caltrans 3 min, Fintraffic 10 min; no flicker (a card draws nothing until its first frame and keeps its last frame through failures). Video cameras stay icon-only. Hovering an icon (pick throttled to 120 ms, paused during camera motion, released after 1 s) summons its card immediately, budget-exempt. The active camera is excluded from cards (it has the monitor plane). Cards tear down completely on disable. |
| Calibration | Poses are estimated priors the user can correct. ADJUST puts a direct-manipulation gizmo on the active camera: heading and pitch rings, east/north/up arrows, range handle at the cap centre, FOV handles on the cap edges. Seven offsets clamp to heading plus/minus 180 degrees, pitch plus/minus 45, FOV plus/minus 50, east/north plus/minus 900 m, mount height -120 to +240 m (minimum 2 m above the ground floor), range scale 0.35x to 3x (quantised to 0.1 / 0.01). Edits are live but unsaved ("CAL . EDITED (UNSAVED)"); SAVE CAL persists them in the browser as a manual calibration (an all-default calibration clears the entry); RESET CAL restores the prior and deletes the entry. Badge states CALIBRATED, CURATED (hand-authored pose), RAW PRIOR. During east/north drags the ground floor is frozen and re-resolved once on release. Typing a value into the readout is also supported. ADJUST does not survive switching the layer off. ### 6.3 Camera coverage and projection mechanism (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]

Plain-words model: each camera is a pyramid of sight with a screen floating at its base.
1. **Pose is an estimate.** Cities publish a position, sometimes a facing direction, almost never tilt, zoom or mounting height. The app invents a raw prior: a facing (from the feed, or a fixed pseudo-random bearing derived from the camera id, one of 16 steps of 22.5 degrees, marked low-confidence and shown as estimated), a downward pitch, a horizontal field of view, a range (distance from the lens to the picture plane) and a mounting height. A manual calibration or a curated (hand-authored) pose overrides the low-confidence flag.
2. **Pyramid.** From the lens, four edges run to the corners of a rectangle at the range distance. Half-width follows from the field of view; height follows from a 16:9 picture shape; the rectangle is tilted by the pitch. That is five points (lens plus four corners). Coverage mode draws them as wire edges; viewshed mode fills the same five points as a translucent solid, each camera in its own hue (hues spaced by the golden angle over the id-sorted catalogue so neighbours always differ). Wires and fill are built from the same points so they can never disagree. Fill is two-sided and not pickable.
3. **Screen.** The latest frame is painted on a 1920 x 1080 canvas and shown on a flat rectangle exactly at the far end of the pyramid. Only one such screen exists at a time (the active camera); idle neighbours get faint wires only, created lazily.
4. **Not burying the screen.** (a) On activation one test ray along the pyramid axis finds the first building or terrain hit and shortens the range to 4 m before it (never below 12 m); a re-activation re-probes from real ground. (b) The screen rectangle is described by nine support points (a 3 x 3 grid: bottom, middle, top rows by left, centre, right columns); their horizontal positions depend only on the pose. The ground height under each is measured, and the plane is lifted by one rigid amount so every support point clears its ground by 2 m (the lift beyond what the mount ground needs is capped at 60 m so a tall building does not push the screen into the sky); a missing measurement falls back to the ground at the mount, so the pre-measurement result is deterministic rather than buried. (c) The screen centre never sinks below 2 m above ground, so a steep pitch cannot bury it.
5. **Ground height is resolved once, not every frame.** Two surface regimes exist: the photoreal 3D city (the globe is hidden and the rendered mesh is the truth) and the terrain globe (the terrain model is the truth). Per camera and per regime there is a one-shot resolved latch: a terrain-height batch gives an ellipsoidal ground prior (waits up to 8 s, then applies late); the first update after 3D tiles finish loading re-queues cameras that were drained while tiles still streamed and refines once from the rendered mesh; catalogue elevations are only a fallback. After that the camera never resamples on a timer; re-selecting a camera re-arms one sample. Precomputed ground heights (mount plus the nine support points, about 3,445 cameras) ship with the app and are valid only while a short hash of the exact pose still matches; otherwise runtime sampling is used. Work drains in small batches (4 cameras per 120 ms, 2 per 250 ms while tracking or cockpit owns the view) so enabling never freezes the frame rate.
6. **No flicker.** A tiny 64 x 36 fingerprint of each new frame is compared with the last and the texture is not re-uploaded if identical; the texture is swapped at most once a second between two alternating canvases because the 3D engine only re-uploads when it receives a new image object. Live video (HLS) uses a decoder whose output the engine reads natively every frame; one decoder per camera feeds both the plane and the panel, the panel copy is bounded to 640 px and 15 fps, live video never loops, and a bounded drift correction handles encoders that publish less media time than wall time.
7. **Calibration.** Each gizmo drag converts to one of seven offsets applied on top of the estimate; a ray-to-axis closest-point and a ray-to-plane computation drive the handles, and a grazing-angle guard rejects near-parallel hits (below 0.08) so one pixel cannot slam a value. Only event-driven picks are used; the ground is frozen during an east/north drag and re-resolved once on release with a revision guard.

Other camera facts added in the census:
- **Operator-defined cameras.** An operator can add or replace cameras through a local file or an inline list; entries override live ones by id (a camera whose live pack stops serving a stable id but keeps an operator entry keeps working) and may be marked curated pose, which shows the CURATED badge and suppresses the estimated tag.
- **Optional protected card for the active camera.** Normally the active camera has only its floating screen; a programmatic option can add a protected, never-faded card. Default off, low value.
- **Media relay hardening.** The relay honours a client Range request only when it is exactly one well-formed byte range (first-last, first-open or suffix), bounded to the same ceiling as the relay's declared-body limit; multiple ranges or line breaks are dropped and the full body returned; a declared oversized body is refused; unbounded live bodies stream with backpressure; a stall timer measures gaps between upstream chunks, not stream life; a departed viewer cancels the upstream request even before headers arrive; a client's own string is never reflected into a health message.
- **Pack and heading provenance.** California elevations arrive in feet and are clamped; every camera image host is pinned to the official origin; Texas ranks by the nearest of several anchors.
- **Assistant surface.** Voice can enable, disable, select by fuzzy name over loaded cameras, next, previous, nearest, focus, coverage, viewshed, adjust, projection and auto hop (refuses with "enable it first" when the layer is off); external assistants get nearest-first area search (facing, feed, credit, "N of M" partial-pack disclosure) and a snapshot returned as an image.
- **Kawn verdict.** SKIP the layer (UAE privacy law); KEEP the techniques (pose-prior with confidence flag, pyramid plus floating screen, lazy neighbour geometry, one-shot ground latch, save-gated calibration) for non-surveillance sensor assets, for example cameras of the owner's own venues or licensed live-stream sources. ### 11.1 How the wind flow works (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13e, "How the wind particle flow works" and gap G1.
1. **Data.** A server step picks the newest published run, downloads only the byte ranges holding the east-west and north-south 10 m wind components (plus optional 2 m temperature and sea-level pressure from the same run and step), decodes them and shrinks the 0.25 degree grid to about 1 degree. The browser receives a small manifest plus raw number arrays. It is a forecast: one frozen moment, not an observation.
2. **Baking the lines of flow, once per field.** Up to 7,200 seeds on desktop (1,200 under 700 px width) are spread evenly by area with a golden-angle spiral (no polar crowding, no repeating pattern). From each seed the wind field is walked both forward and backward, 16 steps each way, each step standing for half an hour, using a two-stage midpoint integration so curved flow stays smooth; each step has a length cap so fast polar winds cannot jump across the map. A path stops at calm air (under about 0.15 m/s), beyond about 88.5 degrees latitude, at the map seam or at missing data; paths under three points are dropped. Survivors are curves of up to 33 points with the seed speed remembered for colour. Baking is not repeated when the camera moves; a scalar change reuses the baked geometry when model, times, grid and wind arrays are unchanged.
3. **Grouping and culling.** Paths are grouped into 30 degree regional cells; each frame, cells behind the horizon or outside the view are skipped, and if none is visible the loop stops until the next camera event.
4. **Animation on the graphics card.** Ribbons sit about 12 km up (a visual aid). One phase value advances; the ribbon shows a bright head with a fading tail sliding along each curve, wrapping about every 6.25 s, with a golden-ratio offset per path so heads are not in lock-step. A faint ghost of the whole curve stays visible (alpha 0.25, 0.34 when paused). Ends, edges and the horizon side fade; ribbons fade under 60 km camera height and vanish under 15 km. No per-point work happens per frame.
5. **Fallback renderer.** Without the graphics path, a canvas renderer moves individual particles: each samples the wind at its location, advances by the wind scaled by camera height (similar apparent speed at any zoom), lives 8 to 12 s, respawns at random spots in the visible region and leaves a trail erased with a half-life of about 0.65 s.
6. **Time.** Forecast time never advances during animation; the run and valid time are fixed until the hourly refresh or a model switch. Model change clears the old field at once, cancels the old fetch and ignores superseded results. The raster colour scales are fixed physical scales (speed 6 stops, temperature 10, pressure 5).
7. **Other.** A wind query (10 m model wind at a point) is available to voice, automation and the situation brief.
- Kawn verdict: ADAPT with a finer regional grid, a Gulf-summer temperature scale and knots as a unit for marine users; the relief-shading helper is LATER. [FROM-DOCS-ONLY for the verdict]

#### 12.4 Timeline mechanism and history depth (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE except where marked]

## 16. Street traffic mechanism and hazard layers (cross-reference, census merge)

The street-traffic layer and the earthquake and fire layers are specified in the movement and event note (03); this section records the mechanism the census added so a rebuilder of the infrastructure and weather layers sees it in one place. Mechanism detail: 13d explainer A and gaps G1 to G5; 13e sections D and E. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]

### 16.1 Street traffic: why the dots look like real traffic
- **Road geometry comes from a map, not sensors.** Below 8 km the layer loads road lines for where the user is looking (the aim point, pulled toward the spot under the camera if gazing at the horizon, never more than 12 km away) from free vector map tiles, plus an operator-keyed commercial flow source when configured. Road source choice: commercial only, map only, or hybrid (no key means map only; with a key hybrid is the default and an explicit choice wins). Two passes: major roads first (always above 4.5 km), then the full network; a re-fetch is skipped when the new view overlaps the old by over 60% and the centre moved under about 350 m; the first dot paint waits at most a quarter second for flow data and otherwise paints plain white dots and recolours on arrival.
- **Colour buckets.** Flow ratio (current over free-flow speed): 0.85 and above free (green), 0.55 to 0.85 slow (amber), below 0.55 jam (red); closed roads spawn no cars; unusable data counts as free flow, never a phantom jam. Honesty rule: colour is real data, white is simulation.
- **Snapping flow onto map roads.** The two sources are cut differently, so they join geometrically. Every flow line is chopped into short pieces filed in a grid of cells about 100 m wide (long pieces subdivided so no cell is missed). Each map road is sampled at up to 7 evenly spaced points; each sample takes the nearest flow piece within 35 m whose compass direction is within 30 degrees of the road's travel direction (a two-way flow line may match either direction; two-way map roads are modelled as two travel directions, each matched alone). A road matches if at least half its samples (minimum two) matched; its level is the median of the matched samples (one bad piece cannot paint a road) and it is closed if any matched piece is closed. Flow tiles are the relative style at zoom 12; features without a numeric level are skipped unless closed.
- **Hybrid de-duplication.** The commercial line wins where it exists; a map road is kept only where it has none. Overlap is tested by sampling the map road every 10 m against flow lines (35 m tolerance, shrinking to 15 m when exactly one of the pair is a motorway or trunk mainline, so service roads 20 to 40 m beside a freeway survive). A partly covered road keeps only uncovered stretches of at least 40 m. Only real drivable flow categories (motorway through minor local) become vehicles or suppress map roads; unknown, private, parking and walking never do.
- **Uncovered roads policy.** White simulated dots (default) or hidden for a strict integrity view; applies on the next camera-driven load and is programmatic only.
- **Cars are simulated, not tracked.** Dot spacing by camera clearance: 30 m under 1 km, 80 m under 3 km, 150 m under 5 km, 250 m above; count per road = length over spacing times a class density factor (motorway 3.0 down to 0.4 for minor roads), a user density scale, a congestion factor (up to 2.5x in slow roads; deeper, up to 4x, in the default live jam mode) and a road weight (two-way roads 0.5 per direction), at least one. Each dot starts at a random place, with speed = class speed x user scale x random 0.7 to 1.3 x congestion factor (never below 0.15). One-way roads use the legal direction; two-way alternate. Near road ends each segment entry has a 0.8% chance of a 2 to 6 s stop (a red-light imitation). A dot reaching the end shrinks away and re-enters as a new vehicle with a new identity; births and retirements fade.
- **Cheap animation.** Each road path is converted once to 3D points with precomputed segment lengths; each frame a dot advances by speed x elapsed time (elapsed capped at 0.1 s so a returning background tab does not teleport cars), carrying leftover metres across corners, placed by straight interpolation (no trigonometry per frame, objects reused). Late-arriving flow rescales speed and density in place without respawning. Dots are lifted a few metres above the sampled surface with heights eased in; budget at most 6,000 dots at close range, shrinking roughly with the square of altitude (floor 400), roads sub-sampled to 80 points, fair per-road share.
- **Jam effects.** Default live jam mode: platoons (anchor at a random distance, 3 to 7 further cars at 6 to 12 m gaps, platoon sizes 4 to 8, one shared direction, wrapping round the road start) with stop-and-go (move 1.2 to 3 s at 2.2x, stand 1.5 to 5 s); jam cars draw a pixel larger with a far-distance scale floor of 0.55. Heat lines (up to 400 corridors, jam 9 px pulsing about every 1.6 s, slow 4 px faint) are a separate default-off option; the owner switch is none, density, heat lines or both.
- **Styled looks.** Dots pass through full-screen look effects. Mono looks (night vision, thermal, noir) use a bright white core with a thin dark halo (jam slightly larger); retro uses saturated primaries with a size boost; normal, anime and snow are untouched; coloured live dots are floored at 5 px under any non-normal look; white simulated dots are never restyled; a switch forces the normal palette; restyle is in place without refetch.
- **Flow proxy and budget.** Commercial tiles pass through a server proxy: the key never reaches the browser, tiles cache 2 minutes, one fetch per tile at a time, and a persistent daily request budget (default 6,000, sized to a 200,000 per month free allowance) after which stale tiles or an explicit budget error are served.
- **Kawn verdict.** ADAPT: UAE roads need a UAE-capable flow provider; keep snapping, hybrid de-duplication, honest simulated/real colouring, the dot budget and the cheap animation. Traffic-light pauses are ADAPT; heat lines ADAPT; jam-mode switch LATER.
