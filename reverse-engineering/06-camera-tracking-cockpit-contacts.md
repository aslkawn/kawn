# 06 - Camera, Tracking, Cockpit and Contacts (behavioural study)

**Purpose.** This document describes how the reference product moves and owns the camera, how a user locks onto a moving object (aircraft, vessel, satellite, fire, camera feed), how the trail and telemetry card behave, how first-person Cockpit mode works (entry/exit, instruments, sensors/visions, controls, weather clouds), how the Contacts roster finds and steps through nearby objects, and the focus policies that decide who may move the camera. It records observable behaviour and numeric limits so a clean-room team can rebuild an original equivalent. Anything not confirmed from source/tests is marked UNVERIFIED.

Confidence tags used below: [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE] confirmed by reading the reference implementation, [FROM-TESTS] confirmed by an automated test, [FROM-DOCS-ONLY] only in project prose or the live walkthrough, [INFERRED] derived. Numeric values in this document were re-checked against the implementation in the revision pass unless a section says otherwise.

Mechanism detail: 13a sections 1 to 3 (search chain, framing modes, sanity gate, building lookup, relative zoom, restore rules) and 13b sections 1.3 to 1.6 and gaps G-06 to G-08 (smoothing, ground floor tiers, gesture rule, tracked camera guard, "nearby").

**Core idea.** There is always exactly one "camera owner" at a time. Any new deliberate navigation, any manual pointer/wheel input, or any new tracking selection takes ownership and releases the previous owner. Automatic things (arrival correction, deferred search flights, shared-link restore) always yield to a human.

---

## 1. Camera model

### 1.1 Basics
- Perspective camera over a true 3D globe. Mouse/trackpad: drag to orbit/pan, wheel/pinch to zoom, right/middle-drag to tilt/rotate (engine defaults; no custom remap other than pinch).
- **Trackpad pinch:** browser ctrl+wheel events are relayed as zoom with an 8x gain, each event's delta clamped to 120 px, so a pinch feels continuous but cannot jump.
- **Zoom limits:** the product sets no global minimum or maximum distance. In the live walkthrough, wheel zooming went from about 36 km to 970 m to 108 m (map tiles stayed blank at ~100 m; stop around 500 m on satellite imagery), and zooming out had no stop (synthetic events reached astronomical heights and a black screen). Programmatic range requests are bounded to 100 m - 20,000 km. While following a tracked object the minimum camera-to-object distance is 150 m and wheel zoom inertia is turned off so the camera cannot overshoot through the target.
- **No keyboard zoom or pan.** The globe has no keyboard camera control at all. The shortcut set is 1-7 (looks), H, O, V, F, D, C, the backtick and Escape, plus Q/W/E/R/T for landmarks, which act only while a city's landmark row is open and keyboard focus is outside text fields and selectors. Arrow keys and plus/minus only act inside the street-level photo viewer. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13a G8] Kawn should add accessible keyboard pan and zoom.
- **Pinch relay edge cases:** see doc 05 section 8.5 (non-pixel and already-relayed events are ignored; nothing is added if the engine already binds ctrl-wheel).
- **Voice relative zoom:** "a little / medium / a lot" move 25% / 55% / 100% of the current distance to the view target (the surface pick at screen centre; if nothing is picked, the camera height, at least 100 m). Minimum step 20 m when zooming in and 50 m when zooming out. Zooming in never goes closer than 25 m to the target and fails with a message when there is no room. It cancels any flight first, and succeeds only if the camera really moved at least 0.5 m; the reply reports requested versus actual metres and the before and after height. Zoom-out cannot reach the full-globe view by itself because of the fractions (a consequence, not a rule); the reset control does that. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13a G6]
- **Pitch:** free orbit allows any pitch; scripted verbs clamp pitch between -89 degrees (nearly straight down) and -5 degrees (just below horizontal). "Straight down" button uses -89; oblique uses -35.
- **Heading** 0 = north. HUD "ONA" (off-nadir angle) = 90 + pitch in degrees (0 when straight down).
- Camera state in a share link: latitude/longitude (4 decimals), altitude (rounded metres), heading, pitch and roll (rounded degrees), plus visual/layer state and at most one tracked target.

### 1.2 Startup camera
- With no share link: the view is first placed at 25 km altitude looking straight down on the default city (Austin in the reference), pauses 0.5 s, then flies 4.0 s (cubic in-out) down to 600 m altitude, heading 15 degrees, pitch -30 degrees. The loader text says it is flying to the default city.
- With a share link: "restoring shared view"; the camera flies 3.0 s (cubic in-out) to the saved pose, then re-applies the exact final pose only if no one else has taken the camera since. A user gesture (pointer or wheel) during the shared flight cancels the delayed camera and any pending shared "follow" selection; the layers/visual state still apply. Recipient intent always beats the link's camera.
- The share link is rewritten (debounced 500 ms) as the camera changes, without adding browser history.
- **Restore rules (census merge):** only a finite latitude and longitude make a link valid, otherwise it is ignored entirely; a missing altitude defaults to 800 m, heading 0, pitch -35, roll 0. Writing of the address is suppressed until the initial restore finishes, so a recipient's first moves cannot overwrite the incoming link. Style, layers, panels, map source and the tracked target restore in separate "lanes" (visual, map, each panel); a later explicit user action claims its lane and wins over a delayed restore. If the manager is torn down during its own flight, it cancels the navigation it owns only if that navigation is still the newest. A link carries a version marker that gates layer, panel and style restore, and a copy-time stamp so recipients can judge how old it is. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13a NAV-36 to NAV-38, G11]
- The loader's flight line names the default city; the splash stays at least 1 s and until the restored style has settled. **Kawn:** the start is Dubai, top-down at city scale (owner decision, docs/09), then the same 4 s descent idea; keep the "recipient intent beats the link's camera" rule.

### 1.3 Ownership, handoffs and refusals
- **Explicit navigation** (city pill, landmark, search result, camera-feed focus, vessel/fire click, reset globe, location jumps): refused with a toast ("Exit cockpit to fly to a <noun>") while Cockpit is active; otherwise it stamps a new navigation generation, releases every follow owner (aircraft, military aircraft, satellite, vessel selection, space-mission camera, Contacts ownership), stops orbit/camera motions, cancels any camera flight and clears any look-at transform, then flies.
- **Deferred navigation** (typed search, which needs a network geocode): stamps a generation on submit without releasing the current owner; when the result returns it re-checks that its generation is still current and that Cockpit is not active, then releases and flies. A superseded or refused search leaves the camera untouched and the old "searched location" readout intact. A failed lookup shows "Location not found" or "Search failed"; a found one with no detailed outline shows "Detailed outline unavailable". The search box closes and clears when the flight starts or the lookup settles.
- **Orientation changes** (tilt, north-up) change the viewing angle without clearing the selection or the follow owner; they stop orbit/motion but keep tracking.
- Layer-owned flights (installation/vessel focus) announce navigation authority so a late geocode cannot land on top of them.
- **When the "world jump" applies (corrected):** detection is suspended and the road-traffic simulation paused only when the user clicks a city or landmark pill while a different preset city is already active. Not on first use, not from a searched location, not for typed or voice flights, not for share-link restores. Reset globe starts its own jump. So a long typed-search flight across continents does not suspend labels. Kawn can apply the rule on flight distance instead. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13a G9]
- A handoff channel notifies listeners (street-level photo follow, etc.) whenever the camera owner changes.
- **Manual input wins:** any pointer press or wheel on the canvas cancels an automatic move (ground-guard lift, arrival correction, camera verbs, routes, orientation animation, shared-link restore). A key press also stops the ground guard.
- Voice/tool camera verbs run through the same policy (one continuous motion at a time).

### 1.4 Top-centre camera cluster
Five buttons, hidden during scene playback (which owns sequencing):
1. **Clear selected layers** - turns off every data layer.
2. **Copy share link** - copies the link (with a fresh timestamp) to the clipboard.
3. **Tilt toggle** - eases the camera (650 ms cubic in-out) about the point under screen centre between straight-down (-89 degrees) and oblique (-35 degrees); heading preserved; "tilted" means pitch above -60 degrees (measured on the orbit frame about the centre target, not the raw camera pitch, so it does not mis-toggle from space). Toast "Tilted view" / "Straight-down view"; the button is exposed as pressed when tilted. While tracking, the orbit is about the tracked object. Rapid clicks continue from the pending destination.
4. **North-up** - eases heading to 0 about the same target keeping pitch; toast "North up"; the compass glyph's needle follows heading live (every rendered frame, because camera-changed events are too coarse), marked "north up" when heading is 0.
5. **Reset globe** - see 1.6.
All four navigate through the explicit-navigation rules (refused in Cockpit). The target under the screen centre is found from the rendered surface first (including photoreal tiles and terrain), then the globe terrain, then the ellipsoid - so plateaus do not swing the camera.
- **Target rule (census merge):** the orbit target for tilt and north-up is the tracked entity's displayed position when tracking (read without advancing the aircraft's own interpolation), else the surface pick at screen centre, else the terrain pick, else the ellipsoid pick. The new view is applied as a look-at about that target and then converted back to a free camera so no hidden transform remains (except while tracking, where the follower's own frame is restored). When pitch is within 1.5 degrees of straight down, the heading is taken from the camera itself because the target-frame heading is undefined there. Tilt is refreshed once at the end of each camera move (the depth read costs about 4 ms, up to 12 ms). The buttons expose pressed state and labels that flip ("tilt map to oblique view" versus "return map to straight-down view"), the north button's label carries the current bearing, and the reset button's label changes while it runs. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13a G12, NAV-33, NAV-34, NAV-70]

### 1.5 Fly-to framing (landmarks and cities)
- Preset cities (Austin, San Francisco, New York, Tokyo, London, Paris, **Dubai**, Washington DC, Tallinn) each hold a curated ground elevation, a view bounding box and five landmarks. Each landmark stores its own framing: camera distance (range), pitch, heading, and an estimated building height used to aim at the building's mid-height.
- Dubai's five presets: Burj Khalifa (range 600 m, pitch -20, heading 200, building height 270 m), Burj Al Arab (500 m, -25, 90, 100), Palm Jumeirah (1,200 m, -40, 0), Dubai Frame (400 m, -25, 270), Museum of the Future (350 m, -20, 30); ground elevation 5 m; viewport box 24.95-25.35 N, 54.9-55.55 E.
- Mechanics: a smooth flight to a bounding sphere around the target at the requested offset (heading, pitch, range), default duration 3.0 s (2.2 s for voice), then a look-at lock so the target ends exactly centred, then the look-at transform is released so manual control returns. If the landmark has footprint bounds, range is the larger of the range that fits the bounding sphere and 1.35 x the sphere radius (exact fitting maths in 1.8).
- The five landmarks are bound to keys Q, W, E, R, T, active only while that city's landmark row is open. The app holds nine cities and 45 landmarks in all; the voice schema lists only eight preset cities (Tallinn is tray-only, though a spoken "Tallinn" still resolves through free text). [13a NAV-24 to NAV-26, G13]
- **Ground height used for aiming** (priority): sampled globe height if above zero -> cached ground-floor from the shared ground service -> curated city elevation -> 0. The next nearby cell is warmed in the background.
- City pill: first click flies to the city's first landmark and expands that city's landmark chips; clicking the same pill again collapses the row; picking another city while one is active makes it an "inter-city jump". Inter-city jumps suspend the detection overlay and pause the road-traffic simulation for the flight (resumed on completion or after a 5.2 s watchdog) so labels do not smear across continents.
- The bottom location tray shows two mini-status lines when collapsed (current city/pin, current landmark). Pills and POI chips are keyboard reachable.
- **Typed search** (location box): an ordered provider chain (coordinates -> bundled names -> Google geocoder if keyed -> keyless open geocoder -> proxied OSM geocoder, ~12 s overall) returns position, name, types and a viewport. Mode is chosen from the types (full table and mechanisms in section 1.8): countries, states and regions fly to the whole viewport; cities frame their viewport after a sanity gate; neighbourhoods and postal codes use close landmark framing; streets fit their box; natural regions (ranges, deserts, seas) use a capped oblique "swath" over the centre rather than flying to space; precise places and landmarks use close landmark framing with a building-outline lookup. A curated landmark whose name is fully contained in the query (at least two significant words) reuses its hand-tuned pose. Explicit exact queries (typed coordinates) are never overridden by a nearby place. Search bias follows the current view, provider by provider (see 1.8).
- **World-click focus** (clicking a vessel or a fire): 1.9 s flight, keeps current heading; framing - vessel: aim sphere 150 m, range 1,200 m, pitch -30 degrees; fire: sphere 400 m, range 3,000 m, pitch -35 degrees. Invalid targets (non-finite or near the Earth centre) are rejected before any tracking is released.
- **Orbit (key O):** needs a current landmark target (otherwise a toast tells the user to fly to a place first). Circles the target at the landmark's range and pitch, 6 degrees per second (about one turn per minute) starting from the current heading; stops on landmark/city change or any new navigation; an indicator shows when active. It holds continuous rendering while active.
- **Camera verbs** (voice/tools): one-shot nudges are bounded (orbit 30 degrees, tilt 15, rotate 15, pan a quarter of view height, 0.9 s). Continuous speeds - orbit 2/6/15 degrees per second (slow/normal/fast), tilt 4/10/20, pan 8/20/45% of view height per second, route dolly mean 20/40/90 m/s. Orbit circles the screen-centre ground point holding range and pitch; manual input or any navigation stops it. The **route dolly** rides at about 260 m above ground, locked 32 degrees look-down, gaze leading the path by about 6.5 s (120-600 m), ease-in/out 2.4 s (never more than 35% of a short route), a banked roll capped at 10 degrees, gentle altitude "breathing" (~20 m over ~2.2 km), 90 m minimum clearance above the rendered ground, maximum descent 10 m/s, terrain pre-warmed up to ~3 km ahead; reduced-motion removes the shaping.

### 1.6 Reset globe
- Button in the top cluster (and a RESET button inside Cockpit). Sequence: stamp navigation, interrupt camera motions, stop orbit, exit Cockpit without restoring tracking, release all trackers (aircraft, satellites, vessels, space-mission camera), clear tracked entity, cancel flights, clear look-at, begin the world-jump transition (detection suspended), then fly straight out to **18,000 km** altitude, pitch -90 (straight down), heading 0, over 2.8 s, **keeping the sub-camera point centred** so the user's continent stays in front. A watchdog (4.2 s) settles the reset as cancelled if the camera did not reach the globe height within 1 km. The button's accessible name changes to "resetting..." while in progress; concurrent clicks return the same pending result. Result reports completion or cancellation and the centred coordinates.

### 1.7 Ground-collision guard
- Arrival framing happens before tiles stream in, so ground height is predicted (see 1.5); a sea-level fallback used to bury the eye in elevated cities (Denver ~1,600 m, Austin ~170 m).
- After a landmark flight completes, a guard samples the **rendered surface** under the framed target and under the camera. Sampling returns nothing until meshes load, so it retries up to **8 times, 600 ms apart**. If the eye is below **120 m** above the highest sampled surface it lifts the camera straight up by the deficit with a 0.9 s ease, holding heading and pitch, so the fix reads as the shot settling. Deficits of 2 m or less are ignored. It stops when both samples succeed, when attempts run out, when the user presses a key, presses/wheels on the canvas, tracking starts, any direct camera API call occurs, or a newer arrival replaces it.
- Cockpit has its own surface rule (12 m minimum clearance, see section 4).

### 1.8 Search and framing in detail (census merge, 13a)

**The four doors.** (1) A city pill or landmark pill/key: instant, no network, using the curated cities. (2) The typed search box: asynchronous, the long path below. (3) A voice or tool request: accepts one of a preset city, free text, a latitude/longitude pair, or a reference to a contact the assistant just listed (relocated at call time because it moves; if it is no longer loaded the tool says so rather than flying to a stale spot); optional close or overview framing, an explicit distance (clamped, not substituted, to 100 m - 20,000 km) and "wait for arrival", which makes the reply come only after the flight lands (arrived) or is cancelled (cancelled, not an error). A refusal because Cockpit owns the camera returns a cancelled result. (4) Reset globe. Wheel, pinch and drag are free-camera controls, not "zoom to a location", and cancel any automatic move.

**Provider chain (first answer wins, 12 s total budget).** In order: (a) decimal coordinates, offline and exact; (b) bundled names, offline: the nine city ids and names and all 45 landmark names, compared after case, accent and punctuation folding, whole-name matches only, so a bundled name never shadows a real place that merely resembles it; (c) the unqualified Alps shortcut, offline, returning the bundled range's centre and box (it raises an error rather than falling back to a same-named peak); (d) Google geocoding if a browser Google key exists, receiving the view as a bounding-box bias; (e) Photon, a keyless open geocoder, receiving only the view centre as a point; (f) the app's own proxy to the Nominatim geocoder, one request per second server-side with a five-minute cache, receiving the box as a parameter (whether the server uses it is UNVERIFIED). Definitive answers (a place, or a clean "nothing") are cached 5 minutes (30 seconds for "nothing"), at most 64 queries per view bias; a timeout or network failure is never cached. Every answer is normalised to one shape: position, label, Google-style types, an "exact, do not second-guess" flag and an optional viewport box, so the framing logic is written once. Bundled names and coordinates ignore the bias.

**Photon adapter.** It asks for five candidates (proximity bias reorders them). Pass one is biased to the view centre and accepts only a candidate whose folded name equals the query or starts with it; pass two is unbiased and takes the first candidate whose folded name equals or starts with the first comma-separated part of the query, else one containing it, else the first. Folding removes accents, lowercases and collapses non-alphanumerics. A malformed candidate voids the whole answer; each request times out at 6 s. Types are inferred from the map-data tags first (parks, stadiums, universities, airports, malls, zoos, theme parks, cemeteries, forests, regions, suburbs as areas; town squares deliberately precise; any natural, water, waterway or land-use tag as a natural feature; road tags as streets) and only then from the coarse class (country, state, county, city, district, street). Reason for tag-first: a lake or park without an area type was framed at building range (the camera ended 26 m above water), while treating a town square as a locality framed it like a city. The label is name plus district, city, state, country with duplicates dropped. The box arrives as west, north, east, south and is dropped, with the place framed at its point, if it wraps the date line.

**Typed coordinates.** Exactly two decimal components separated by a comma, a semicolon or whitespace (whitespace only where the next token starts a number or hemisphere letter). Each may carry one hemisphere letter N/S/E/W before or after the number and an optional degree sign; a sign together with a letter is refused. Without letters, latitude comes first; with letters either order is accepted if the two letters name different axes. Refused (falling through to the geocoders): out-of-range values, more or fewer than two components, trailing text, degrees-minutes-seconds, grid references and plus codes. The result is flagged exact, labelled with four decimals and hemisphere letters, and given a box about 3 km across, so it frames as a precise place unless a range is passed. Kawn can add DMS and plus codes later.

**Rescue of a mis-read name (only when the answer is not flagged exact).** The app finds the point under the screen centre (the point beneath the camera if the centre shows only sky). If the geocoder returned nothing, or its answer lies more than 50 km from that point, a Places text search biased to a 6 km circle around the view centre is asked, and its hit is trusted only within 8 km of the view centre; it then replaces the point, label, types and box, cached per query and rounded centre. Example: "the Capitol" typed over Austin means the Texas State Capitol, not the one in Washington. A near geocode, a typed coordinate or a bundled name is never overridden. Without a Google key this step quietly does nothing. [UNVERIFIED whether a keyless substitute exists]

**Framing mode from the place type.**

| Place type | Mode | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| country, state or province, county | region overview | fly to the whole padded box (countries skip the sanity gate) |
| city or town | city overview | fly to the box, after the sanity gate |
| district, neighbourhood, postal code | neighbourhood close | close landmark framing at 250 m with no building-outline lookup |
| street, intersection | street corridor | fit the box; with no usable box, 900 m range |
| park, natural feature, campus, university, airport, stadium, amusement park, zoo, cemetery, mall | area overview | fit the box (huge natural boxes use the swath rule); with no box, 1,400 m range |
| anything else (building, address, point of interest, bare coordinate) | precise place | close landmark framing, default 250 m, with the outline lookup |

An explicit distance from the caller skips box fitting and uses landmark framing at that range. An explicit "close" forces landmark framing even for areas; an explicit "overview" forces box fitting even for precise places and switches off the sanity gate. A bare voice coordinate flies to a 250 m, -35 degree, heading 0 view over the point in 2.2 s. [13a section 1.5]

**Box fit.** The box is padded by 12% of its height and width, minimum 0.05 degrees each way, latitude clamped to 89.9. Width is measured the short way round the planet so a box crossing the date line is not read as 340 degrees wide; padded edges are wrapped back into range (a crossing box becomes an east-less-than-west rectangle) and a padded width of 360 degrees or more becomes the full longitude range; the reported centre uses the same rule. The engine flies to the rectangle, choosing the altitude so it fills the view, looking straight down with north up. Duration is 3 s for the UI, 2.2 s for voice, cubic in-out, and the end transform is reset so an old look-at lock cannot bend the flight. Without these rules a 0.4-degree metro box straddling the date line frames about 87 degrees of ocean. [13a G10]

**Sanity gate for administrative boxes (cities and states or provinces, not countries, not explicit overviews).** Administrative boxes can far exceed the place meant: "Tokyo" is a prefecture including islands about 1,000 km out to sea. Rule: if the box diagonal exceeds 300 km (111.32 km per degree of latitude, short-way longitude) and the geocoder's anchor point lies further than 15% of the diagonal from the box centre, the box is replaced by a 40 km square around the anchor (latitude capped, longitude wrapped). Measured ratios: Tokyo 0.40 and Hawaii 0.46 are gated; Nunavut 0.10, Alaska 0.09, Japan 0.09, Texas 0.05 and California 0.04 keep their boxes, so 0.15 sits in a 2.2x gap. Countries are exempt although France (0.39), Portugal (0.38), Ecuador (0.30), Chile (0.26) and Spain (0.22) show the same flaw from overseas territories; the authors flagged and left it. Acceptance: "Tokyo" ends within about 50 km of Tokyo Station. **UAE:** emirate-level boxes (Abu Dhabi emirate includes large desert and offshore islands) very likely trip the same flaw; test "Abu Dhabi", "Dubai" and "Sharjah" and keep the rule. [UNVERIFIED for the UAE]

**Natural-region swath.** A natural feature whose box diagonal exceeds 400 km (Rockies about 2,700 km, Alps about 880 km; Lake Tahoe at about 50 km stays below) would put the camera in space. Instead the camera frames an oblique swath: centred on the box centre, 280 km range, pitch -35, heading north for a feature taller than wide and east for one wider than tall, so the range recedes along its long axis. Kawn: Hajar Mountains, Empty Quarter and Arabian Gulf as bundled regions.

**Landmark framing (precise places, explicit ranges, pills).** (1) Ground height of the aim point: first the sampled terrain height if above zero, then the shared ground-floor service's cached value, then the city's curated elevation, then zero; the next nearby cell is warmed in the background; for a searched place the app waits (bounded) for the ground-floor lookup so an uncurated hill town is not framed against sea level. (2) Building outline, precise places only: ask the map-data service for buildings, man-made structures and tourist attractions within 180 m of the point and score candidates: 1,000 points per query word longer than 2 letters shared with the feature's names, plus 500 if the point is inside the footprint, minus the distance in metres. Footprints under 2 m in either dimension are ignored. Height is the tagged height, else 80% of the longer side clamped to 12-80 m. If the source is unavailable the flight still happens and the user sees "Detailed outline unavailable". (3) Range: with an outline, a bounding sphere of half the 3D diagonal of the building box times 1.18; the camera range is the larger of the range that makes that sphere fill 57% of the limiting viewport dimension (real field of view and aspect, times 1.05) and 1.35 times the radius; without an outline, the caller's range (250 m default; each preset landmark carries its own). (4) Pitch: preset landmarks use their own; searched buildings by shape: height over footprint of at least 2.5 gives -12 degrees, at least 1.2 gives -22, at most 0.35 gives -45, otherwise -32; no outline gives -25. Searched heading is 30. (5) Aim height is ground plus half the building height (plus 30 m for an unknown landmark, 0 for a bare coordinate). (6) Flight and look-at lock as in 1.5. (7) Ground guard as in 1.7. [13a G5]

**What the user sees.** The search box gains a "searching" look; toasts "Location not found" and "Search failed" on the two failures; the tray's collapsed two-line readout switches to the searched address (first segment as place, the rest as context, or "Searched location" for a one-segment answer) and the previous city highlight and landmark row are cleared; a preset city always wins over a searched label in the readout; the box closes and clears when the flight starts or the search settles. Search is a state machine (idle, searching, found, missing, failed, cancelled, disposed); a new search aborts the previous one; a stale result is dropped before any camera or UI effect.

**Shared ground-height service.** A coarse grid of about 111 m cells over a batched terrain-height resolver: synchronous cache reads on the render path, misses fetched in 64-point sequential chunks (cache of 20,000 entries, geoid-math fallback if a chunk fails), lift 1.5 m, rendered-mesh samples accepted from 15 m below to 80 m above a real elevation prior, corridor warm-up up to 12 cells. The aircraft tiers built on it are in doc 03 and 13b section 1.6. [13a NAV-39]

**Error and message catalogue for this domain.** "Exit cockpit to fly to a location/target"; "Location not found"; "Search failed"; "Detailed outline unavailable"; "Fly to a POI first" (voice: no active landmark to orbit); "Camera is already at the minimum target distance"; "camera position did not change"; map messages in doc 05; tool errors for an unknown map source, unknown motion, missing direction, and a flight request with no place. [13a G14]

**UAE / Kawn.** Only Dubai is a curated city. Add Abu Dhabi (Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, Etihad Towers, Louvre Abu Dhabi, Yas Island), Sharjah, Al Ain, the other emirates and the airports from the owner location tray (docs/09: seven emirates, DXB, DWC, AUH, SHJ, and London airports as a secondary group); add Arabic aliases to the bundled-name index; replace the public Photon and Nominatim services with licensed or self-hosted geocoders with Arabic and English handling; Arabic-script queries are untested in the reference. An untagged building height is clamped to at most 80 m, which would under-frame supertall Dubai towers; Kawn should keep curated heights for its landmark list and rely on tagged heights elsewhere. [INFERRED]

## 2. Click-to-track and trail

### 2.1 Selecting
- Click an aircraft glyph/model (civil or military), a vessel, a satellite, a fire, a camera feed, etc. While Draw mode holds the pointer, clicks go to the drawing tool instead. Clicking a trail line does nothing (it never reads as "empty space"). Clicking empty space, pressing Escape, the contact aging out of the feed, disabling the layer, or a voice "stop" all deselect.
- **Escape is shared by many owners, resolved by who sees it first** [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]: Draw mode takes it first and consumes it (cancels the shape in progress, or leaves Draw if none; nothing else reacts); the Cockpit takes it next while active (closing the credit lightbox, an open radio or Display popover, the Contact window or the briefing window one step at a time before exiting); the attribution lightbox closes itself and returns focus to its link; panels with a focused disclosure collapse; and finally each selectable layer (tracked aircraft, military aircraft, satellite, vessel, transit vehicle, bike-share dock, street-level pin) releases its own selection in place. Search closes on Escape too, a running scene stops on Escape ("Stopped (Esc)"), the recent-imagery box tool consumes it to cancel its drawing, and the directions tool disarms or clears its step selection. Clean UI and recording mode have no Escape behaviour of their own.
- **Click versus drag (gesture rule, census merge):** a click counts as a selection when pointer travel stays within 6 px (press duration is ignored, so a stationary long press can select). A deselecting click on empty space needs both travel within 6 px and press duration within 400 ms. Travel is accumulated segment by segment, so an orbit nudge that returns to the start pixel cannot pass as a click. Pick rules: a pick registry resolves heterogeneous ids (hex strings, numeric satellite ids, vessel objects); a pick that belongs to a sibling layer is not empty space; clicking the aircraft that is already tracked (via its 2D entity, its standalone model, or any pick carrying its address) is a no-op; a tool that owns the pointer (Draw) blocks track requests; when markers stack, military wins over civil over local, tracked on top. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13b AIR-67, AIR-68]
- **Deselect never flies:** the camera stays exactly where it is, immediately free (an earlier pulled-back overview on deselect was rejected as "loses my context"). Tracking release is in-place.
- A tracked target is encoded in the share link as "at most one tracked target"; on opening the link the follow restores after the camera flight and is a handoff, not a bookmark; recipient gestures cancel it.

### 2.2 Aircraft follow camera
- On select: any camera flight is cancelled, the tracked entity becomes the camera subject, and the camera is placed once at a computed offset "behind and above" the aircraft: follow range = clamp(altitude x 1.1 + 2,500 m, 3,000 m to 30,000 m); the offset is 0.8 x range behind and 0.55 x range above (default altitude 1,500 m if unknown). After that first framing the engine's own follow logic is the only camera writer (two writers caused jitter); a small guard enforces the 150 m minimum range and prevents crossing through the target. Reference frame is east-north-up so the camera does not flip.
- The aircraft icon keeps drawing the display position used by the camera (not a fresh dead-reckoned sample), so labels do not swing around the icon at close range. **Corrected in the census merge:** the camera, rotation, label and trail head share one cached display position per frame, but the tracked card anchors to a separate "visual position", the translation the 3D model really renders with, including the ground-snap offset (up to about 100 m inland). The two differ when a grounded model is shown; the card must sit on the aircraft the user sees. [13b AIR-71]
- **Tracked camera guard details:** any camera flight is cancelled first (otherwise voice-started tracking mid-flight would not frame). After the first framing the engine's own entity-follow is the only camera writer (two writers produced sub-pixel jitter). The guard enforces the 150 m minimum range, turns the camera back if a programmatic zoom crosses the target, switches zoom inertia off while tracking and raises the zoom controller's minimum distance to 150 m, restoring both on release. The framing logic runs before the render, not after, because changes after the render made camera and icon disagree for a frame (a visible front/back oscillation). The follow frame is fixed to east-north-up because an automatic frame alternated with the model's own frame. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13b AIR-70, G-07]
- **Position regimes (summary):** the fleet is drawn one poll interval in the past so a position is an interpolation between two real fixes; a newly seen aircraft is extrapolated backwards (up to 60 s) until history accumulates; a paused feed coasts forward on a constant-rate-turn arc (at least 60 s, capped at 5 minutes, drawn at 45% alpha); displayed course is rate-limited (60 degrees per second, scaled down at low speed). Rule for a rebuild: the delay equals one poll interval. Full detail: 13b section 1.3 and doc 03.
- Aircraft motion is rendered about 30 s behind real time (one poll interval) and interpolated between two known fixes, so icons glide rather than snap; the tracked display absorbs any discontinuity (feed handoff, backfill splice) into a correction that decays over 900 ms with zero steady-state lag.
- Live stale cue: if the contact has missed polls or the feed is in backoff, the card appends a stale marker and the aircraft coasts on dead reckoning.

### 2.3 Billboard to 3D model handoff for the tracked aircraft
Detailed in doc 05 (7.3): model within 150 km camera altitude, back to icon beyond 172.5 km; the cyan glyph remains until the model is ready. A tracked model scales down if the camera is extremely close so it never fills the screen (cap about 200 px).

### 2.4 Trail
- A polyline behind the tracked aircraft, uniform opacity 85% (40% where it passes behind or below scenery so it never vanishes), width 2.5 px, always visible (an earlier tail-fade was removed on owner request: "show actual tracks"). Colours: civil aircraft #00d4ff, military #FFB800, vessels #39ffd5. Grounded tracked aircraft also get a trail (taxi/roll history) on select.
- Seeded at selection with the short dead-reckoning history, then back-filled asynchronously from the OpenSky track history (waypoints older than the oldest local fix, capped at **400 vertices**, newest kept). Seeding uses only fixes at or behind the delayed display time (newer fixes join as they age past the delay), and during aircraft warm-up nothing is drawn. The civil backfill is a fire-and-forget request to the provider's experimental per-aircraft track service (its own credit bucket, about 4 credits per call, 60 s server cache of 200 entries); waypoints carry time, latitude, longitude, barometric altitude, track and an on-ground flag, and only those strictly older than the oldest seeded fix are spliced ahead of the local segment. The military history uses a per-aircraft trace file from the community aggregator (undocumented upstream, up to about 24 h). Backfill failures (404/429/timeout/malformed) silently leave the local-only trail. [13b AIR-59, AIR-60, AIR-24] Low baro segments are floored to the ground surface (resolved within ~1.2 s, never blocking) so the trail never dives below the mesh; with no baro and no resolved floor the previous altitude is held.
- The trail body excludes the newest raw fix (which is a poll interval ahead of the delayed icon); a live head segment bridges the last body point to the displayed icon each frame, and is suppressed on a parked contact so no line runs through the fuselage.
- Other layers: transit trails fade by age (about 80% newest down to 8% by 15 minutes); vessels cap at 400 points with a 25 m minimum move; satellites show an orbit ring (red line in the walkthrough).

### 2.5 Tracked telemetry card
- A tracked card, composited above all other overlay cards, shows: title line = callsign (else registration, else hex) - altitude (feet, or FL for 18,000 ft and above) - speed in knots - STALE if applicable; second line = airline and type; third line = origin to destination airport codes when a plausible route exists. Updated live (voice narration reads the same live values). Cached altitude on the ground can be stale (observed 1,700 ft at 81 kts on a runway).
- The HUD's bottom-right vessel line shows a selected vessel's name, type, speed, heading, MMSI and position time (see doc 07).

### 2.6 Other trackable things
- Satellites: camera rides at orbital distance with an orbit ring and telemetry (satellite layer; see layers doc). Space missions have their own camera owner released by any other navigation.
- Fires and vessels: click fly-to (1.5 world-focus above); a tracked fire or vessel offers a one-click jump to the nearest live camera feed (see 5).

## 3. Contacts (roster)

### 3.1 What it is
Context mode "Contacts" (tab in the right-rail Context panel; the other tab is Space Missions) stages a nearby-objects picture around one **subject**: the currently tracked aircraft, a selected vessel or a selected mapped installation. Tab help: "cycles the nearest contacts of whatever type you select - planes, vessels, installations; satellites track independently". Entering Contacts turns on its dependency layers (civil flights, military flights; vessels and installations are loaded on demand), turns detection on at the tactical preset, and exiting restores the user's exact prior layer set and detection state (layers the user added during the session are kept; entry layers are not re-enabled).

### 3.2 Radius and refresh
- Window: **250 km** around the subject (labelled "FLIGHT / VESSEL WINDOW" with the subject name).
- **What counts as "nearby" (census merge):** an aircraft is included in nearby counts, the Contacts roster and analyst proximity only if it is tracked, or its billboard is shown, or its 3D model is genuinely rendering. A model that exists but is hidden, unplaced or still loading does not count; suppressed duplicates (the civil copy of a military aircraft) and aircraft hidden behind the horizon are excluded unless a hidden-inclusive query is requested. A rebuild that counts every record will disagree with what the user sees. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE, 13b AIR-79, G-08]
- Re-evaluated every 750 ms while parked, every **175 ms** while the camera pose is changing (owner complaint that arrows lagged), returning to the slow cadence once the pose has been unchanged for 250 ms; also re-evaluated when the subject moves 250 m.
- Mapped installations load around the subject (not the camera view); the installation layer moves its window only after the subject travels a long way.
- Cohorts: civil flights (source OpenSky), military flights (adsb.lol), vessels (AISStream), mapped installations (OpenStreetMap). A cohort whose feed is down or stale reports "unknown" (count shown as "?"), never as zero; zero means a healthy empty feed. Count text reads "at least N" when the query cap was hit. Per-cohort source and coverage text are shown under the cohort name, with a reason string.
- Safety wording at the bottom: open-source observed/mapped context; missing broadcasts, unloaded map areas or unmapped sites are not evidence of absence. Cockpit footer repeats "not an all-clear".

### 3.3 Panel contents
- Header: subject label and "250 KM" scope; named areas expander when the subject lies inside named regions.
- Three buttons: **PREVIOUS**, **FOCUS**, **NEXT** (PREVIOUS disabled at the start of history, NEXT disabled when no path exists). FOCUS re-frames the camera on the current subject.
- One section per cohort: name, count (with page number), source/coverage/reason, and **3 nearest entries per page** (each a button reading label plus distance; distances under 10 km show one decimal). Pages auto-rotate every **10 s** per cohort with more than three entries. Up to 10 examples are kept for display; navigation walks the whole in-range cohort (up to 10,000 entries; query ceiling 20,000).
- Labels follow the layer convention callsign, else registration, else hex. Pressing an entry flies to and selects that contact.
- Compass overlay on the map: a ring inside the keyhole showing heading ("HDG nnn") with N/E/S/W labels rotating with the camera, and up to three direction arrows on the rim - one per entry on the current page of the subject's own cohort (falls back to the military cohort), each with a short label and bearing; arrows fade with the Cyber sonar if active.
- While Contacts is active the compass is hidden when no subject or results exist; the panel then shows a standby message ("select a flight, vessel or mapped installation").
- Contact lost: when the subject leaves its feed the panel keeps rendering last-known data with a "contact lost" cue and PREVIOUS/NEXT remain operable.

### 3.4 Filters
- Navigation can be restricted by target layer (civil flights only or military only), by aircraft class (substring match against class, type code or type name - for example helicopters only), or aircraft-only (voice/Cockpit use). The UI buttons use no filter; filters arrive from voice and tools. "Helicopters only" works from the same nearest-first ordering.

### 3.5 Stepping logic (NEXT / PREVIOUS)
1. PREVIOUS walks backward through **visited history**; evicted contacts in history are skipped.
2. NEXT first moves forward in history if possible; otherwise picks the **nearest unvisited contact** inside the 250 km window (subject-first stable order), recording the visit.
3. If every in-window contact has been visited (and the subject is an aircraft and its cohort is known), NEXT **expands the search by doubling the radius** from 250 km up to 16,000 km (whole-Earth chord) to find the nearest unvisited aircraft anywhere; if none, the visited cycle resets and the search repeats.
4. NEXT with an unknown subject cohort (feed down) can only use forward history.
5. FOCUS on entering Contacts picks the contact nearest the current view (civil and military compete in one pool, military first on exact ties); an AIS vessel is a fallback only and never assumed military; there is deliberately no distance cap.
6. History is bookmarked by layer and id and is session-scoped.

### 3.6 Detection tie-in
Activating Contacts forces detection on at the tactical preset (Dense, 75%) if it was off; it stays on through Cockpit enter/exit and third-person tracking; the user may turn it off inside the session and it stays off; deactivating restores the earlier mode and density - unless the current look auto-owns detection (NVG/FLIR/CRT preset), in which case the look's preset stands. Links copied during Contacts carry the saved pre-Contacts detection values, not the forced ones.

## 4. Cockpit mode (first person)

### 4.1 Entry and exit
- **Entry button** ("COCKPIT", aeroplane icon) lives in the Contacts view and is visible only when a tracked aircraft exists **and Contacts is active (and not mid-start) with both civil and military flight layers on**. Otherwise entry is refused with a stated reason (Contacts starting, Contacts inactive, no tracked aircraft). Key **C** also enters (and exits). Voice can enter, retargeting to a requested layer/class first. **Key C is shared with the camera-feed layer shortcut**: the Cockpit key handler sees the key first and decides. With Cockpit active it exits. With an aircraft tracked it takes the key (entering when the entry gate is open, otherwise swallowing it silently, for example when Contacts is not active); with no aircraft tracked it ignores the key and the general shortcut toggles the camera-feed layer instead. The Cockpit handler ignores the key with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd held, while typing in a field, and on key repeat; the general shortcut handler has no modifier check, so a copy shortcut pressed outside a text field also toggles the camera-feed layer (a quirk a rebuild should not copy). A spoken or tool entry attempt while the gate is closed is refused with a plain reason: either that Contacts is still starting, or that Contacts must be opened first. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- **Entry is a transaction:** the tracked target is adopted, the cockpit entered, and on any failure the previous tracker is rolled back; the explicitly attempted selection is undone through the same persistence path so the share state does not keep the failed target. Cockpit cannot be carried by a share link. [13b AIR-85, AIR-125]
- On entry: any camera flight is cancelled; the tracked aircraft's own marker is hidden (you are inside it); the map camera inputs are disabled; the continuous-render hold is taken; the body gets cockpit styling; the standard map panels (data layers, camera feed, weather, imagery, street level, scenes, Display, Context, Radio) are collapsed out of the way; the first-person overlay appears; a "track acquired" signal row is pushed ("<callsign> - course nnn"); the briefing carousel starts on page one and the vision chip is set from the current map look; focus moves to the exit control.
- **Narrow windows (760 px or less):** the cockpit still runs, but the map HUD text, the left context window, the right signals window and the map rails are hidden; the Display/Radio launchers move to a row at the lower right, and the EXIT COCKPIT and RESET buttons sit as pills at the bottom centre. At wider sizes the HUD text stays visible at about 78% size and 72% opacity in the four corridors around the optical centre. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- Exit: Escape (the first Escape closes any open sub-popover - radio dock, Display/Radio popover, Contact panel, briefing panel - before the next Escape leaves), **C**, the EXIT COCKPIT button, or RESET (also goes to the globe). On exit: vision restored, camera inputs re-enabled, the aircraft marker shown again, tracking restored (follow camera re-applied) unless leaving via reset; focus returns to the entry button. If the tracked aircraft disappears from its feed the cockpit exits automatically, except for a 1.5 s grace after a Contacts NEXT/PREVIOUS so hopping does not drop out.
- Contacts NEXT/PREVIOUS inside Cockpit are restricted to aircraft; the newly selected plane becomes the new cockpit (marker swap, heading reseeded, anchor reset).
- Key shortcuts are ignored while typing in a field. Cockpit-mode status is broadcast so other modules (detection, Cyber theme) adapt.

### 4.2 Camera
- The eye sits **7 m ahead of and 2.6 m above** the aircraft's reference point, looking along the heading with a fixed **-4 degree** pitch. Heading follows the reported ground track, slewed at no more than 28 degrees per second.
- Position is advanced inertially each camera tick (every 50 ms) from reported course and speed rather than snapping to each delayed feed update; it converges on the layer's displayed position at a bounded rate (about the larger of 0.75 m/s and 22% of ground speed) so a late fix never causes a surge or reversal. With a stale feed it holds exactly on the rendered position.
- **Ground rules:** the eye keeps at least 12 m of clearance (minus the 2.6 m offset) above a floor taken from the shared rendered-surface sampler; a confirmed ground contact waits for the photoreal surface under it to be sampled (probing every 500 ms for up to **5 s**; the map camera stays put and the meta line says "acquiring surface", then falls back to a flagged "surface fallback"). With no floor known and speed under 90 m/s the aircraft's own clamped height minus the lift is used.
- HUD/instrument updates every 100 ms; contact/briefing updates every 250 ms.
- The own aircraft is never drawn in first person; nearby traffic remains silhouettes and far traffic pips (doc 05). **Corrected:** the silhouette-versus-pip split uses the same add and keep radii as 3D model admission (150/185 km in Proximity, 400/450 km in All), independent of whether models are enabled, loading or capped (doc 05 section 7.2). In cockpit, detection omits only the active cockpit subject; a subject present in both the civil and military feeds is suppressed in both. [13b AIR-88, AIR-117]

### 4.3 Instruments (first-person overlay)
- Frame lines and a central scan array of concentric rings, tick arcs and corner marks (pure decoration) around a my-location reticle; roll arc (-30..+30 in 10s), left and right pitch rails (-20..+20), a "level" horizon guide.
- Top line: "first person", callsign (else registration, hex), and a meta line: fleet (military/commercial), feed state (live track / stale feed / acquiring surface / surface fallback), "course aligned", and an emergency tag when the contact broadcasts an emergency (states what was broadcast, never a conclusion).
- Speed and altitude **tapes** on the left and right rims, curved to follow the circular keyhole: nine ticks, moving smoothly behind a fixed pointer; ground speed in knots with ticks every 10 (below 100), 20 (below 300) or 25 kt; altitude in feet with ticks every 100 (below 5,000), 250 (below 15,000) or 500 ft, labelled in five-digit padded form; speed three-digit padded. Confirmed ground contacts read 0 ft. Missing values show dashes and the tape greys.
- Large readouts: ground speed, heading (three digits plus degree sign, circular roll), altitude; digits roll up/down per character; compass tape of seven divisions 30 degrees apart centred on heading with N/NE/E/SE/S/SW/W/NW labels at cardinals.
- Clock (UTC, seconds) and lat/lon to 3 decimals.
- **Estimated flight plan card:** FROM and TO airports (code and name), a status ("arrow - estimated direction" or "route data unavailable"), and a **destination arrow** on the visor showing relative bearing to the destination (clamped to +/-120 degrees) with label "DEST nnn degrees". Hidden when no route is known.
- **Vision chips:** PREV / CURRENT / NEXT cycle Normal, CRT, NVG, FLIR, Anime, Noir, Snow (wraps); label text uses NORMAL/CRT/NVG/FLIR/ANIME/NOIR/SNOW.
- **Utility launchers:** DISPLAY (opens a popover that hosts the same HUD toggle/layout, detection controls, shared look-parameter sliders and 3D models controls) and RADIO (compact player). A cockpit-specific layout algorithm keeps these clear of the REC readout and the briefing panel (minimum top offset about 96 px or 12% of height).
- Help line: ESC exit, C toggle.

### 4.4 Contact panel (cockpit)
Subject line (name and "250 KM", optionally installation coverage), previous/next/collapse buttons, four cohort counts (FLT, MIL, AIS, SITE) with sources, the nearest observed/mapped object with a rotating direction arrow, bearing relative to the nose (AHEAD within 8 degrees, otherwise L/R nnn degrees; dashed when the subject is not the aircraft itself) and distance, a footer with uncertainty text ("n inputs unknown - not an all-clear" or "available inputs current - not an all-clear"), an updated clock, and the weather-effects switch. "Context standby" appears when no snapshot exists. Collapsing is remembered per session; Escape collapses the focused panel first.

### 4.5 Briefing carousel
Three pages with next/previous, SIG/NEWS/LOCAL tabs and an auto-cycle button (OFF by default; when on, advances every **9 s**, pauses while hovered or focused):
1. **Live signals** - up to five rows, newest first, each timestamped in UTC: the current subject (track), nearest civil and military flights as actionable buttons (name and distance, selecting one switches the cockpit), an input-unknown warning row when feeds are unknown. Sources are the proximity engine; text says source-backed events, no synthetic news.
2. **Regional news** - latest location-matched headlines via a news RSS query, with age ("12M AGO", "3H AGO", "2D AGO"); refreshed every 5 minutes or when the aircraft has moved 25 km; status text while acquiring.
3. **Local info** - place name and coordinates, temperature, wind speed with 8-point direction and degrees, sky condition and cloud cover, precipitation (Open-Meteo credit link, "natural earth" place names).
The panel can be collapsed and re-expanded by the user (user choice is respected).

### 4.6 Cloud and weather effects ("WX")
- Optional switch in the contact footer (OFF by default; the preference persists in browser storage). When on, a transparent full-screen cloud layer draws only while Cockpit is active and never touches the map or the engine's fog/post-process stages.
- Data: current conditions for the camera position from the weather service, refreshed every **5 minutes** or after the aircraft moves **25 km**; a first refresh about 0.45 s after entry.
- Weather code chooses the effect family; numbers set strength. Cloud strength = max(cloud cover mapped over 8-100%, a forced floor: 0.92 for thunderstorms, 0.7 for rain or snow, 0.55 for fog). Rain/snow floors and precipitation (0.05-8 mm) set precipitation strength; fog and visibility under 12 km set fog; wind 4-90 km/h sets drift speed and direction. A missing weather code produces no effect (fails clear, never invents conditions).
- Altitude fades: precipitation fades out between 6 km and 14 km, cloud between 14 km and 24 km, haze between 9 km and 20 km.
- Rendering: three octaves of noise, 24 ray-march steps, drawn at about **12 fps** into a buffer capped near 520 x 320 pixels, softened at the bottom of the visor; strength eases toward its target (8% per frame; instant under reduced motion); visible only above a small threshold. The pass halts while the browser tab is hidden. If the GPU context cannot be created the effect reports unavailable.

## 5. Camera-feed (CCTV) focus policies

- Camera-feed controls: Nearest, Previous, Next, a camera dropdown, Focus, Coverage (cycles off -> on -> viewshed), Auto-hop (interval configurable between **8 and 90 s**), Projection (draped live image on/off), and pose calibration (heading, pitch, field of view, range with click-to-edit values).
- **Flight durations:** Nearest 1.8 s, Previous/Next 1.4 s, dropdown pick 2.2 s, Focus button 1.9 s, clicking a camera marker in the world 1.9 s.
- **Gate:** every one of these buttons first enables the camera layer if needed; if the user clicks something else meanwhile or the active camera changes underneath, the stale action is dropped. Selection and flight use the explicit-navigation rules (release trackers, refused in Cockpit with the toast).
- **Layer enable focus:** turning the camera layer on activates the nearest camera but **flies only when no aircraft tracker and no Cockpit owns the view**; ownership is sampled before and after the awaited enable and either observation suppresses the flight (the camera is still activated and shown).
- **Programmatic activation and auto-hop never fly** the camera (only world clicks and explicit buttons do). Auto-hop panel behaviour: the panel only expands on first activation so it does not pop open on every hop.
- A world click on a camera marker activates it only if activation really changed something, then requests the same cockpit-safe focus.
- Refresh cadences: frame snapshot about every 10 s while active, 60 s when idle; projection 10 s active / 60 s idle; health sync every 7 s.
