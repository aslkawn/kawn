# 03 - Data layers: movement and events (behavioural reverse-engineering)

Purpose: this note records, layer by layer, what the studied browser globe application does for every moving-object layer and every event layer: where the data comes from, how often it refreshes, the hard limits, what the camera/viewport does to the layer, how markers are coloured, what the click/track card shows, which filters exist, what happens on failure and what key or credential each one needs. It is written so a clean-room team can rebuild an original product (target: Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah; Arabic + English, right-to-left) without reading the original source. Everything is behaviour; numbers are the values observed in the studied product and are sizing hints, not requirements. Anything not confirmed is marked UNVERIFIED.

Confidence tags used per section: [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE] read directly in the product's behaviour-defining code, [FROM-TESTS] stated by its automated tests, [FROM-DOCS-ONLY] only in its README/docs, [INFERRED] reasoned, not seen. Unless a section says otherwise it is [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE] and, where tests exist, consistent with them.

Pass 3 (census merge) added mechanism-level explanations (sub-sections numbered x.1 and onward inside sections 1 to 10) from the three census notes: air domain in 13b, space and sea domain in 13c, ground-level domain in 13d. ## 0. Cross-cutting rules that every layer in this note obeys

### 0.1 Layer panel and row presentation [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- All layers live in one collapsible "Data Layers" list on the left rail, split into six groups in a fixed order that does not depend on startup order. Movement: Satellites, Live Flights, Military Flights, Local ADS-B, Live Vessels, Street Traffic, Transit, Bike Share. Cameras: Cameras, Recent Imagery, Street Level. Infrastructure: Mapped ALPR Cameras, Mapped Installations, Data Centers, Submarine Cables, Dams. Events: Space Missions, Earthquakes, Active Fires, Fire Perimeters, GNSS Integrity. Weather: Wind, Rain radar, Satellite clouds, Lightning, Cyclones. Utilities: Directions, Radio. Row titles worth knowing: the launches row is titled with a "(30d)" suffix, the earthquakes row with a "(24h)" suffix, and the vessel row reads "Live Vessels" in the panel.
- Each row has an on/off control and a one-line status. An enabled row shows a normalised health word instead of a bare "ON": ON (nominal), LOADING, DEGRADED, STALE, PARTIAL, FALLBACK or UNAVAILABLE. The metadata line beneath keeps the human reason (source name, age, count, error text, next retry).
- Health derivation rules worth copying, in the order they are applied: an explicit unavailable/offline/down/error status wins; an error (or "not available" flag) with no prior data reads UNAVAILABLE, but an error with prior data reads DEGRADED (the old data stays drawn); loading overrides what follows; "guidance" statuses (zoom in, run a search) read as normal ON, not as faults, unless the cache is genuinely stale; a source that is a declared fallback (including the community aggregator used when the primary aircraft feed fails, anything labelled fallback, or simulated traffic) reads FALLBACK; stale beats degraded; PARTIAL is for incomplete-but-honest coverage.
- A row that needs a free provider key but has none shows UNAVAILABLE with "KEY REQUIRED" and a hover/accessibility text naming which key to add in the in-app settings panel; a layer that declares a missing optional key does not make the global "loading" chip show a failure.
- Only a few rows carry sub-controls (chips, legends, info line): Satellites (DENSE chip plus class legend with live counts), Street Traffic (road-source chips), GNSS Integrity (info line plus three-band legend with counts), Fire Perimeters (four-band legend with counts). Flights, military, local ADS-B, vessels, transit, bikeshare, launches, earthquakes and active fires have no row chips. The 3D toggle and Proximity/All mode live in the separate Display panel, not on the rows.
- Layers are off by default; only a first-run mission card (Live Contacts, Space Missions, Environmental, Explore manually) switches sets on, and only after the user picks one. The Environmental mission enables earthquakes and fires together. Layer on/off state is saved in the browser and written into the share link with a permanent one-character token per layer; tokens are never reused, even if a layer is removed.
- Enabling is a transaction: a layer that fails to enable returns to its previous state and shows an error toast; rapid on/off toggles are serialised and the latest request wins.

### 0.2 Honesty rules (the product's strongest idea) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- A dead feed must never look alive. Items older than a layer-specific age are removed or visibly faded, not frozen. Retained rows from before an outage never turn a dead-feed status green.
- Snapshot freshness uses the source's own timestamp, not the time the proxy received it. An old snapshot reports its true age.
- Counts always name their scope (in view, loaded, within a radius). Missing broadcasts and unmapped sites are never evidence of absence; this is stated on cards and legends.
- "Guidance" messages (zoom in, search first, enable a key) are distinct from errors.

### 0.3 Rendering and interaction conventions [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- Smooth motion for moving layers is achieved by rendering slightly in the past and interpolating between two real, timestamped fixes; if no next fix exists, the object coasts by dead reckoning for a bounded time, then is marked stale and finally removed. Display latency is an intentional product decision. The local receiver layer is the one exception: it shows aircraft as heard, with no display delay. Vessels are the other exception in the opposite direction: they have no interpolation or dead reckoning at all and step to each new report (section 5.4). Full aircraft smoothing mechanism: section 1.3. Satellites are not interpolated either; they are recomputed from orbital elements (section 6.1).
- Icons rotate to heading using the current camera basis so rotation is correct at any tilt; far-side-of-planet objects are horizon-culled.
- Labels/cards are chosen greedily by priority with about 150 px minimum screen separation and a per-layer cap; selected items always get a protected full-detail card that is never faded or capped. Cards fade by distance and at the screen "keyhole" edge, and are hidden in cockpit mode where noted.
- Click selects; a selected moving object can be tracked (camera follows at a comfortable standoff, minimum tracked range about 150 m). Only one tracked subject exists at a time across aircraft, military aircraft and satellites; selecting one releases the others. Clicking empty globe or pressing Escape clears the selection. Vessels, transit vehicles, local ADS-B aircraft, launches, fires and bikeshare stations are selectable but the camera does not follow them (local ADS-B is explicitly never followed).
- Heights: all objects sit slightly above the terrain or sea surface (vessels 3 m above the local sea surface, ground dots 3 m above road surface, bikeshare stations 2 m) using a shared ground-height lookup that is warmed only at close range; at national zoom everything rides the plain ellipsoid. Aircraft altitude from barometric sources is converted through a geoid model.
- Shared share-link state carries: layer on/off, a few allowlisted options (aircraft 3D mode, selected aircraft/satellite id, satellite catalog), and a tracked target. A tracked target that has not arrived yet shows an "acquiring" progress state until a source-specific deadline, then fails with a message.
- Every layer registers a data credit into one expandable attribution panel that stays visible in clean-view and recording modes.
- DETECT overlay membership (sampled subset of objects shown with tactical brackets): flights and military (uncapped candidate list), satellites, vessels, street-traffic dots, bikeshare stations, cameras and transit; each other layer is capped at about 2,600 candidates and military is weighted highest (1.4), traffic 1.15, cameras 1.1, bikeshare 0.9. Active fires expose a detectable list but are not yet wired into the overlay (the code says so); earthquakes, perimeters, GNSS and launches do not take part. [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]

### 0.4 Context (Contacts) coupling [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
- A separate right-rail "Context" panel, not a data layer, offers two mutually exclusive modes: Contacts and Space Missions. Contacts switches on civil flights, military flights, vessels and installations (only if the user has not already enabled them) and builds a 250 km "context window" around a selected aircraft, vessel or site: counts and nearest examples per type, three examples per page rotating every 10 s (at most ten examples kept), with Previous / Focus / Next controls, a compass ring with up to three bearing arrows, and a refresh every 750 ms while parked (175 ms while the camera is moving; 750 ms is also the subject refresh while it moves). Space Missions mode switches on launches and satellites and isolates every other layer (restoring the prior set on exit). Directions, Transit and others are switched off while an exclusive Context mode is active and restored afterwards. The context layer is internal (named "Global Context", hidden from the layer panel) and takes its aircraft from the OpenSky-sourced and adsb.lol-sourced layers, vessels from the AIS layer and sites from the installations layer. Full behaviour is in the Contacts note.

### 1.1 Position streams and the feed-state contract (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b section 1.1 and gap G-09.
- The air domain has four independent public streams and two local ones, none needing a user key: the worldwide civil snapshot (30 s browser poll, one shared server snapshot of per-aircraft state rows, not filtered to the viewport), the regional fallback (a circle of about 250 nautical miles around the view anchor, converted to the same row shape), the military list (15 s poll), and the two local streams of section 3 (browser radio and decoder feeds, one-second reads).
- The server is the only party that talks to the upstream providers. The browser learns the source name, coverage wording (for example a 250 nm regional fallback), row count, authentication mode and failure reason, and cache status and cache age from response metadata, not from the body. Fallback responses name themselves.
- Freshness is taken from the source snapshot's own time, not from when the browser received a cached success. A snapshot more than 2 minutes old, or without a time, counts as stale: the layer backs off, shows "Source snapshot N min old" (minimum 2) and marks every contact STALE. A cold start straight into a rate limit with nothing cached is answered with a locally made rate-limit reply rather than a silent hang.
- Rows are reduced to one internal shape with units converted. Callsign, speed and track are sticky: a later poll lacking the field never erases the last good value, but a changed value always wins. Emergency status is deliberately the opposite (per poll, not sticky, apart from one source-switch omission) so an old 7700 cannot linger. These rules, the landed fast-cull and the removal counts were each added after a field defect; copy them exactly.
- Landing rule correction: the fast-cull constants (one missed poll when the last fix was below about 150 m barometric, about 500 ft, and under about 23 m/s, about 45 kt; three-poll grace otherwise; 20 s back-off) are one shared rule for civil and military, not two. The feed's on-ground flag lags the real touchdown by about a minute, which is why the rule exists (it removed phantom aircraft at airports).

### 1.2 Classification, enrichment and card honesty (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b rows AIR-41, AIR-42, AIR-51 to AIR-56 and gap G-05.
- Classification order: first, ICAO type-designator sets tested in turn for fast jet, drone, helicopter, four-engine, widebody, turboprop, glider, business jet and light; a known type that matches none of these becomes airliner. With no type, the live extended category number is used (2 light, 3 to 5 airliner, 6 widebody, 7 fast jet, 8 rotorcraft, 9 glider), then the emitter-category letters (A1/A2 light, A3/A4 airliner, A5 widebody, A6 fast jet, A7 rotorcraft, B1 glider); otherwise airliner. Each class has a readable label for the card ("Widebody", "Fast jet", "Drone"). 3D scales are clamped 0.75 to 1.45 while models are shared.
- The public card presents the default airliner class as if it were fact before enrichment arrives; the local-receiver card has an evidence flag that prevents this. Kawn should add an explicit unknown class so the card never presents a default as fact.
- Style changes (for example a thermal sensor look) re-tint the whole fleet's models: they are dropped and rebuilt 40 per tick with the billboard shown first, because releasing hundreds of GPU-backed models in one handler stalls rendering. Cockpit entry follows the same rule: never destroy models synchronously.
- Model assets are authored to real-world metres with the nose toward a fixed local axis (so a 180 degree heading offset applies), origin at the bounding-box centre, with a recorded belly offset (1.4 to 7.8 m) and bounding radius per asset. A billboard remains the visual until its model is loaded and placed, so there is never a gap or a double image.
- UAE: add widebody types (A380 and similar) and Gulf business-jet types to the sets, and a UAE fleet table (local registry, A6- tails) as an enrichment source.

### 1.3 Smoothing: three position regimes (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b section 1.3 and gap G-03.
The fleet is drawn one poll interval in the past (30 s civil, 15 s military), keeping each aircraft's last 5 fixes. This is the central idea: with real fixes on both sides of the displayed instant the position is a plain interpolation, so there is no extrapolation error and no snap-back when the next poll lands. Rule for a rebuild: delay equals one poll interval. If Kawn polls a regional feed every 5 to 10 s the delay shrinks to about that interval.
1. Interpolation between two fixes: linear in Earth-fixed 3D. Displayed course blends the reported track (itself interpolated in time, so a slow turn advances continuously) with the direction of the chord between the fixes, weighted by ground speed (at hover the chord is only GPS noise; helicopters always use the reported track).
2. Warm-up for a newly seen aircraft: the displayed time predates its first fix, so the oldest fix is extrapolated backwards (up to 60 s) along its own velocity; as history accrues the icon glides into normal interpolation with no freeze and no backward jump. The tracked trail draws nothing during warm-up.
3. Coasting when the feed pauses: the newest fix is extrapolated forward on a constant-rate turn arc if a turn rate is measurable, otherwise a straight line, for at least 60 s past the last position (longer while contact messages keep arriving, never beyond 5 minutes), drawn at 45% alpha. The geometry is local east-north-up rather than repeated latitude and longitude trigonometry, for per-frame cost.
- Course smoothing: displayed course is rate-limited to 60 degrees per second (scaled down at low speed, a stalled frame can spend at most 0.25 s of elapsed time in one step), hiding the step at each fix boundary.
- Tick rates: the whole fleet advances about every 80 ms; rotation recomputes only when the camera pose changes or once per second while idle. The tracked aircraft advances every frame, and one cached position per frame is shared by the icon, rotation, trail head, follow camera and card so they cannot disagree.
- Discontinuity absorber for the tracked aircraft: a jump larger than a plausible per-frame move (real speed times frame time times four, plus 25 m) becomes an offset that decays to zero over 900 ms; steady-state lag is zero.
- Icon rotation is computed by projecting the course vector into screen coordinates whenever the camera moves, so the nose is right at any pitch; near straight-down views a screen-aligned rule with hysteresis takes over. A billboard behind the planet's limb is hidden by an explicit horizon test, because the globe is hidden in the photoreal look and no depth test exists to hide far-side planes.
- Draw and pick order when markers stack: military above civil above local, tracked on top; a trail line click is claimed as a no-op so it never reads as empty globe and deselects.

### 1.4 Altitude datum chain (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE; geoid ranges INFERRED]
Mechanism detail: 13b section 1.5 and gap G-02.
The globe needs height above the reference ellipsoid, while feeds give barometric altitude (a pressure datum, roughly mean sea level, weather-dependent) and for some aircraft geometric GNSS altitude. Render height is chosen in this order: (1) geometric altitude as-is; (2) barometric altitude plus the local geoid undulation from a bundled global grid (about 2.7 MB, loaded lazily, undulation about minus 106 to plus 85 m worldwide), labelled a visual fallback and never claimed exact; (3) for a grounded aircraft with no altitude, the cached ground height at its cell (current cell first, last poll's cell second, because a taxiing aircraft is always one cell ahead of the warm batch); (4) last resort for a new grounded contact with no data, the geoid surface itself (right to within metres at sea-level airports such as the coastal Gulf fields); (5) otherwise hold the previous render height, so a one-poll barometric dropout cannot snap the plane down by the geoid offset and back. The aviation altitude (barometric feet or flight level) stays separate and is what labels, route plausibility and camera range read. Kawn note: UAE airports sit at or near sea level, so the geoid step and the ground floor matter more than any photogrammetry offset; rule for a rebuild is that every tier fails toward "slightly high" rather than "buried".

### 1.5 Ground floor tiers (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b section 1.6. Five cooperating mechanisms keep ground traffic on the ground.
1. Coarse floor grid: ground height cached per cell of three decimal places of a degree (about 111 m); render-path reads are synchronous cache lookups only, the poll loop warms cells in batches; anything below floor plus 1.5 m is lifted to it so a sprite sits on the surface instead of fighting it.
2. Rendered-mesh floor (photoreal look): the visible photogrammetry sat about 17 m above bare-earth terrain at one US apron, so terrain-floored sprites buried themselves. A one-shot sampler measures mesh height per coarse cell under strict rationing: viewer within 15 km, nothing if the camera is above 25 km, at most 40 samples per poll, one shot per cell per session, an undefined result (tiles not streamed yet) is not latched, and a sample is accepted only between 15 m below and 80 m above a warm terrain prior (a vertical probe can hit a rooftop or another aircraft). Mesh answers first, terrain second.
3. Held floor and ease: a contact standing on a borrowed neighbour floor keeps its last resolved floor while within about 1 km of where it was measured, probing adjacent cells at most every 500 ms; a floor that drops is approached exponentially (time constant 360 ms, at most 22% of the remaining gap per tick), a rise applies at once. A retired floor seed stays reusable 90 s because the on-ground flag flaps through a take-off rotation while the height source switches.
4. Ground snap for 3D models: a depth-tested model cannot use the billboard's ignore-depth trick, so each grounded modelled aircraft gets one successful vertical sample of the tile skin, cached per aircraft, invalidated beyond 50 m of movement, retried with exponential backoff (max 30 s) and budgeted per window so a freshly modelled airport cluster cannot fire dozens of picks in one tick. Until it resolves the model is withheld and the billboard stays; the belly offset is added so wheels sit on the surface.
5. Corridors: for ground-moving or coasting aircraft within 25 km, cells along the path two poll intervals ahead are pre-warmed (at most 64 new cells per poll, 4 per aircraft in the first pass).
Known residual: at a freshly visited airport, grounded and low aircraft float low for one to two polls (30 to 60 s). Cockpit mode waits up to 5 s for the surface under a confirmed ground contact. No UAE measurement of mesh offsets exists; tuning figures come from US airports [UNVERIFIED for the Gulf].

### 1.6 Click gesture, follow camera and "nearby" (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b rows AIR-67 to AIR-71, AIR-79 and gaps G-06 to G-08.
- Selection gesture: a click selects when pointer travel stays within 6 px (duration is ignored, so a stationary long press can select). A deselecting click on empty space needs both travel within 6 px and press duration within 400 ms. Travel is accumulated segment by segment, so an orbit nudge that returns to the start pixel cannot pass as a click.
- Pick rules: a registry resolves mixed id kinds; a pick belonging to a sibling layer is never treated as empty space; clicking the already-tracked aircraft (via its icon, its model, or any pick carrying its address) is a no-op; a tool that owns the pointer (drawing) blocks track requests.
- Initial follow framing: any running camera flight is cancelled first; the camera is placed once behind and above the aircraft in a local east-north-up frame (range = altitude x 1.1 + 2,500 m clamped 3,000 to 30,000 m; 0.8 of range behind, 0.55 above; 1,500 m assumed if altitude is unknown).
- Follow guard: after first framing the engine's own entity-follow is the only camera writer (two writers produced sub-pixel jitter). The guard enforces a 150 m minimum range, turns the camera back if a programmatic zoom crosses the target, disables zoom inertia and raises the zoom controller's minimum distance to 150 m while tracking (both restored on release). Framing logic runs before render, because after-render changes made camera and icon disagree for a frame; the follow frame is fixed to east-north-up because an automatic frame alternated with the model's frame.
- Card anchor: the camera, label, rotation and trail head share the one per-frame display position, but the card anchors to a separate visual position (the translation the 3D model really renders with, including the ground-snap offset, up to about 100 m inland) so it sits on the aircraft the user sees.
- Eviction versus deselect: an aircraft aged out of the feed is distinguished from a deliberate clear; the cockpit contact panel holds last-known data after an eviction. Deselect restores the billboard with its last rendered rotation and does not move the camera.
- Definition of "nearby": an aircraft counts toward Contacts counts, the roster and analyst proximity only if it is tracked, drawn as a visible billboard, or really rendering a 3D model (a model that exists but is hidden, unplaced or loading does not count; suppressed duplicates and horizon-hidden aircraft are excluded). A rebuild that counts all records will disagree with what the user sees.
- Silhouette versus pip in cockpit mode uses the same add and keep radii as 3D admission (150/185 km proximity, 400/450 km all), independent of whether models are enabled.

### 1.7 Trails and history backfill (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b rows AIR-57 to AIR-66.
- One polyline per trail (the owner rejected per-vertex tail fading: "show actual tracks"); uniform 85% alpha, 2.5 px, dimmed to 40% where hidden behind scenery by a depth-fail material, so it never disappears; consecutive points closer than a small distance are merged.
- Because the icon is drawn one poll behind the newest fix, the trail body is every accumulated fix except the newest, and a per-frame head segment joins the last body point to the delayed icon (needs at least two accumulated points; suppressed on a parked aircraft). At selection the trail is seeded only with fixes at or behind the delayed display time.
- Civil history comes from the upstream provider's experimental per-aircraft track service (its own credit bucket, about 4 credits a call, 60 s server cache, 200 entries; waypoints carry time, latitude, longitude, barometric altitude, track and an on-ground flag). Waypoints strictly older than the oldest seeded fix are spliced ahead of the local segment, capped at 400 vertices keeping the newest; any failure silently leaves the local trail. Track altitudes are barometric only, so render height is barometric plus geoid with a bounded 1.2 s terrain-floor resolve; a waypoint without altitude sits on the surface if the floor is known, else holds the previous height; leading points with nothing to carry get a 10 km default. Whether the provider's track service is available to free accounts at build time is UNVERIFIED.
- Military history uses a per-aircraft trace file served by the aggregator (keyed by address, undocumented but live, no browser cross-origin access so it is proxied, 60 s cache, 200 entries, 5 MB cap, 12 s timeout, attribution required).
- No replay: the product contains a retained report-time playback engine (compact fixes, shared 32 MiB budget, seek, rate, gap markers, outlier quarantine) used only by transit. Aircraft have trails and an analyst track tool of 200 thinned points, nothing more. Kawn: flight replay for incident review is high value and could reuse that design.

### 1.8 Emergency handling details (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b rows AIR-103 to AIR-110.
A squawk must be four octal digits to count; 7500 is unlawful interference, 7600 radio failure, 7700 general (international convention [INFERRED]). The aggregator's emergency field is a superset (general, lifeguard, minimum fuel, nordo, unlawful, downed, reserved). Emergency severity ranks general, unlawful, nordo, downed above priority severity (minimum fuel, lifeguard, which keep normal colour); highest wins, on a tie the ADS-B field wins, and a 7x00 squawk counts even when the ADS-B field says none. Wording depends on whether the Mode A code itself names the status ("broadcasting squawk 7700 (general emergency)") or the ADS-B field does ("ADS-B reports minimum fuel"). The primary snapshot carries squawk but no emergency field, so its provenance differs from the fallback's while the kind agrees. Local radio feeds cannot detect emergencies (the decoder reads extended squitter only, no squawk; decoder-feed records could carry squawk but the card does not show it); Kawn's receiver network should carry squawk. There is no alert list, sound, notification or history; a cheap, high-value Kawn addition is an operator alert feed. UAE note: the transition altitude is 13,000 ft in the UAE [INFERRED, general aviation knowledge], so the card's flight-level switch should follow it, not the 18,000 ft convention.

### 3.1 How a radio frame becomes a position: the in-browser decoder (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE; standards INFERRED]
Mechanism detail: 13b gap G-01 and rows AIR-27 to AIR-30. Class caveat: the class shown on a local aircraft is only as good as the emitter category (identification messages) and the type lookup; the decoder supplies no squawk and no quality fields.
- Hardware session: the browser talks to the dongle over WebUSB, reading blocks of 32,768 samples. A read not finished in 5 s is treated as a stalled transfer and the session is torn down; a graceful close is bounded at 2 s, then the raw device is closed (overall twice that bound). The tuner runs at 1,090 MHz, 2.0 megasamples per second, fixed gain 28.0 dB by default; the browser USB library maps decibels to tuner stages differently from common desktop tools (20.7 dB heard about 1 message a second, 28.0 dB about 9 on the same antenna). The browser remembers the receiver identity per mode (FM versus ADS-B); dual-channel boards appear as two USB devices that can share a serial number, so the channel named for ADS-B or 1090 MHz is preferred for ADS-B and a channel not named for ADS-B or UAT for FM; an already-authorised device opens without the picker. Real tuning: gain matters enormously and an antenna tuned for 1090 MHz matters more than software.
- Step 1, magnitude: each unsigned 8-bit I/Q pair is centred, squared and summed into one power value per sample; two samples make one microsecond, which is one Mode S bit.
- Step 2, preamble: a frame starts with 4 pulses at offsets 0, 2, 7 and 9 of a 16-sample preamble; the six gaps between them set a local noise floor (the last six samples are deliberately unused because pulse tails leak into them on real 2 Msps hardware). A candidate needs pulse-minus-quiet contrast of at least 120 and a pulse mean at least 1.8 times the quiet mean; the bit decision level is quiet mean plus 0.35 of the contrast.
- Step 3, bits: 112 bits follow, two samples each; a bit is 1 if the first half-sample is stronger than the second; if the halves differ by less than the larger of 20 and 2% of the contrast the bit is too weak and the frame is dropped.
- Step 4, validity: downlink format must be 17 (extended squitter) or 18 (non-transponder or ground relay) and the standard 24-bit Mode S parity check must leave a zero remainder. Failed frames are discarded; no error correction is attempted, unlike some desktop decoders.
- Step 5, continuity: one frame minus one sample is carried over between USB reads so a frame split across a block boundary is recovered.
- Step 6, fields: the aircraft address sits in bytes 2 to 4 and a 5-bit type code selects the content. Types 1 to 4: 8-character callsign in a 6-bit character set plus emitter category (A7 rotorcraft, A1 light). Types 5 to 8: surface position with a 7-bit movement field mapped by bands to speed (0.125 kt steps near zero up to 5 kt steps near 100 kt; the top value means 175 kt or more), optional ground track in 360/128 degree steps, no altitude. Types 9 to 18: airborne position with barometric altitude (Q-bit set: 25 ft steps minus 1,000 ft). Type 19 subtypes 1 and 2: ground-speed velocity (supersonic multiplier of 4) giving speed and heading, vertical rate in 64 ft/min steps. Not decoded: GNSS-height positions, airspeed subtypes, aircraft status (so no squawk and no emergency), operational status (so no navigation quality locally), short replies.
- Step 7, position: compact position reporting packs latitude and longitude into 17 bits each, alternating even and odd frames with different zone sizes (15 latitude zones). A global decode needs an even and an odd frame within 10 s (25 s on the surface, where movement is slow). A single frame is decoded locally against a reference known to be within half a zone: airborne uses the aircraft's own last accepted position if under 10 minutes old, else the receiver location; surface zones are a quarter of the globe so the reference must be within 45 nautical miles, only the aircraft's own recent position qualifies and the receiver location never does (distance cannot be inferred from one surface frame); a surface pair has four candidates 90 degrees apart and the nearest the reference wins, with no reference meaning no answer. Airborne and surface frames never pair.
- Step 8, sanity: every fix is checked against the last accepted fix (1.5 times reported speed plus 50 kt; 1,000 kt when unknown; 350 kt for light, glider and rotorcraft; 500 m allowance). A failing fix is counted and dropped; from the third consecutive rejection a globally decoded fix becomes the new reference so one bad accepted fix cannot freeze a track. A surface message sets on-ground and clears altitude and climb; the next airborne position clears it.
- Step 9: tracks with no valid message for 60 s are removed.
- Decoder-feed safety additions: the feed host name is resolved before every read and the connection is pinned to the validated addresses (loopback, private-range, localhost and .local only; redirects refused), in addition to the rules in the table above.
- Local-receiver emergencies: none are available (see section 1.8).

### 4.1 Evidence aging and rendering details (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13b gap G-04 and row AIR-38.
- Presence and degraded evidence age separately: a degraded report keeps that aircraft degraded for a full 30-minute window however many healthy reports follow, but a recovered aircraft is not held degraded forever. Rows are dated by when the proxy observed the snapshot, so replaying a cache never restarts a window and a snapshot older than the window adds nothing.

- Cells are rendered as non-pickable fills; a ground primitive with picking disabled still swallows picks of overlapping geometry, so a render-only fill is used so other ground items stay clickable.

### 5.1 Data cleaning and static merge (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c rows SEA-23 to SEA-26, SEA-31 and gap G-09.
- Subscription: the whole-globe box with five message kinds (class A position, class B position, extended class B, ship static and voyage, class B static).
- Sentinel clean-up: AIS "not available" codes become empty values instead of nonsense on screen: speed of 102.3 knots or more (code 1023 in tenths) is a sentinel, course 360 means not available, and a heading outside 0 to 360 (code 511) is dropped. Cards then show placeholders for speed and heading.
- Static merge: static reports are stored per vessel number separately and merged into the live row, so a vessel first seen by position is named "MMSI n" until its static message arrives (up to about 6 minutes for class A, longer for class B). Names come from metadata first, then the message; type and destination fill only if empty.
- Chevron scale rises with speed (0.6 below 8 knots, 0.68 from 8, 0.78 from 18); one icon image per colour is reused; drawn through terrain with horizon culling. A ship 3D model file ships with the assets but nothing uses it [INFERRED from absence].

### 5.2 Feed watchdog details (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c row SEA-28. The watchdog judges by valid AIS records, not handshakes: stale after 2 minutes of silence, socket killed after 5 minutes (2.5 times that), backoff 5 s, 15 s, 60 s, 5 minutes then a 15-minute slow retry. An authentication rejection is terminal and probed hourly; a key change clears it; a rate limit honours the provider's retry hint. Termination is hard rather than polite, so the single connection slot is freed. Checks run every 15 s and on each browser request; the silence watch arms itself only for the worldwide subscription.

### 5.3 Truncation, area filtering and the 12,000-row ceiling (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c rows SEA-27, SEA-29 and gap G-10. The server holds up to 50,000 vessels, drops rows older than 30 minutes with their tracks, returns rows newest first and cuts to the requested maximum (the browser asks for 12,000). In a busy world the cut silently drops the stalest vessels, which are the slow or moored ones that report rarely. Kawn: filter by bounding box on the server (Gulf and Hormuz), and when the world exceeds the cap sort by distance to the view. Whether the hosted version of the product filters by area server-side is UNVERIFIED.

### 5.4 Absences to treat as Kawn decisions (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c rows SEA-36, SEA-38, SEA-42, SEA-50 and gap G-11.
- No motion between polls: no dead reckoning or interpolation exists for vessels (unlike aircraft); positions step when a new report arrives, so the cadence is "step, not smooth". Kawn: extrapolate from speed and course.
- No ports: no port polygons, berths, anchorage areas, port calls or arrival lists; only the free-text destination from the ship's own report. Kawn: Jebel Ali, Khalifa and Fujairah anchorage overlays with dwell and arrival counts.
- No cable-to-ship tie: no ship-near-cable warning, anchor-risk zone or ship-in-corridor alert. Kawn: a Gulf cable-cut risk story using cable corridors.
- Nearest-camera cross-link note: UAE port cameras are not public.

### 6.1 How a position is computed (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE; accuracy statements INFERRED, general orbital-mechanics knowledge]
Mechanism detail: 13c section 1 and gaps G-01 to G-05.
- Inputs: one element set per satellite, a short two-line record republished by CelesTrak from the US tracking catalogue. It states the epoch it describes, the orbit shape (stretch, tilt against the equator, where the tilt line points, closest point, where along the orbit the satellite was at the epoch), orbits per day, and a small drag term. The browser clock supplies the current time.
- Calculation: the standard SGP4 model (deep-space variant for orbits of 225 minutes or longer, such as GPS and geostationary) rolls the orbit forward from epoch to now in the browser. It is a fast formula, not a simulation: it includes Earth's equatorial bulge, average drag and, for high orbits, Sun and Moon pull. No server computes positions.
- Outputs: position and velocity in a non-rotating Earth-centred frame, converted to longitude, latitude and height above the ellipsoid by the Earth's rotation angle (sidereal time). Speed shown is the magnitude of the non-rotating velocity (about 7.7 km/s for the ISS), not ground speed.
- Cost: the core fleet of about 840 is recomputed in about 1.6 ms per pass. The tracked satellite uses one shared clock reading per frame so dot, label, camera and speed readout cannot disagree.
- Accuracy limits: a fresh element set is good to about a kilometre at epoch; error grows with age, typically a few kilometres a day in low orbit, much more after a manoeuvre (an ISS reboost or Starlink orbit raise). A day-old ISS set is usually right to seconds for a naked-eye pass, a week-old set can be off by tens of seconds to minutes; geostationary and GPS orbits are far more stable. The product never checks element age and warns only when the proxy served a stale copy because CelesTrak was down. Other simplifications: sea-level observer, spherical Earth of 6,371 km for the shadow test, no atmospheric refraction, brightness and weather ignored.
- Orbit ring construction: 181 points evenly spaced in time over one orbital period (from orbits per day), all with one frozen Earth angle so the loop closes instead of drifting west by about 24 degrees. It is a 3D orbit shape, not a ground track. It is baked once as a single line primitive built synchronously (an earlier asynchronous rebuild every second blinked); every second the whole ring is rigidly rotated about the polar axis by the Earth's rotation since the bake, keeping it locked under the live dot with no recalculation. The tracked ISS holds under 1 km from its ring.
- Ring styling: ISS ring red, tracked satellite ring yellow; 60% alpha in front of the Earth and 35% where hidden behind it (depth-fail look); width 2.5 px for the ISS, 2 px otherwise; not pickable, so clicks pass through to satellites or deselect.
- Dot scaling by distance: every dot scales with camera distance, 1.5 times at 1,000 km down to 0.6 times at 20,000 km. The ISS label has priority 1000, full size inside 1,000 km and 0.4 times at 30,000 km, is horizon-culled, and is hidden while the ISS is tracked or docked to whatever is tracked. Legend counts are cached against a catalogue revision number (avoids scanning about 10,700 entries per panel refresh); a class with no members is omitted.
- Tracked marker and camera: a short-lived yellow 14 px dot with white outline replaces the dot, drawn through terrain; follow offset is 450 km west, 450 km south and 350 km up (about 726 km), four times that above 2,000 km altitude. Other dots near the followed one dim through a gated alpha pass (at most every 80 ms, with a floor so dots never vanish). Share-link restore uses a pending-restore intent with a generation counter; a partial catalogue can prove presence but never absence.
- Lookup: voice and tool follow-lookup takes the exact catalogue number else the first entry whose name contains the text, with no ambiguity check, unlike the pass lookup (exact number, exact name, then substring; more than one match returns up to 8 candidates and the total). Kawn should use the ambiguity-safe rule everywhere. "Satellites" always means the data layer, never imagery, through the voice alias table. Analyst records: up to 2,000 on demand, core first; distance is ground distance, not line of sight.

### 6.2 Pass prediction maths (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c rows SAT-28 to SAT-33. Scan forward in 20 s steps until elevation passes the threshold (default 10 degrees; if already above at the start, the pass begins now); bisect the crossing 10 times for sub-second rise and set; walk the pass in 5 s steps for the peak and refine it with a parabola through three samples; out-of-range inputs (latitude, longitude, horizon beyond 72 hours) are rejected. Visibility: Sun position from a low-precision solar formula (about 1 arcminute); sunlit unless inside a cylindrical Earth shadow (no penumbra); observer dark when the Sun is at or below minus 6 degrees; visible if any sampled instant above the threshold is both sunlit and dark, with shadow and twilight crossings resolved to 20 ms. The "sunlit" and "observer dark" flags in the answer describe the peak only, while the visible flag covers the whole pass. The external tool fetches a fresh group (default stations; seven groups allowed, including the Starlink one), appends "orbit data may be stale" when the proxy served an old copy, and returns a suggested follow view at 3,000 km. A sibling tool lists satellites above a minimum elevation right now, highest first, with compass direction. No pass panel exists on the globe: Kawn should build a pass table for Dubai and Abu Dhabi (ISS and Gulf-visible passes).

### 6.3 Absences and the celestial ring (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c rows SAT-35 to SAT-41 and gaps G-03 to G-05.
- Real time only: every computation uses the browser clock; no time slider, no future positions, no past replay of satellites (only the launch replay has its own timeline). SGP4 could compute any instant, so a Kawn time scrubber is cheap.
- No ground track, no sub-satellite trace, no coverage circle and no "visible from here" cone; the ring is the 3D orbit.
- Sun, moon and stars: the celestial ring (an optional thin ring around the circular scope with a gold Sun marker and a cyan Moon marker) contains only the Sun and Moon; there are no planets (the older "sun/moon/planet" wording was wrong). Directions come from the globe engine's built-in low-precision planetary model, rotated into Earth-fixed axes by the inertial-to-fixed matrix (simpler fallback available) and projected on the camera's right and up axes; the marker angle is the arctangent of those components. When a direction is nearly along the view axis the marker keeps its last bearing and fades to a 28% minimum opacity. It is a direction in the camera plane, not an observer sky chart. The ring shows when the Earth disc clears the scope by 24 px and hides below 12 px; the toggle turns itself off when the disc is not fully inside and no focus flight runs; it works only in the Normal look, resamples every 60 s from real time, caps at 30 fps with canvases of at most 2 M pixels and pixel ratio 1.25.
- The 3D scene has no custom sky box, sun, moon or star code, no day/night shading and no terminator line; the Earth is lit evenly (only atmosphere brightness and saturation are set). Whether the engine's default stars are visible at globe scale is UNVERIFIED. A terminator would suit the pass-visibility feature.
- UAE: the six core groups likely omit national satellites (KhalifaSat, MBZ-SAT, Thuraya, Yahsat/Al Yah, Etihad-SAT); add the full active group so they can be named and followed, plus a national class [INFERRED].

### 7.1 Matching, estimates and replay timing (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13c rows SEA-07 to SEA-20 and gaps G-06 to G-08. UAE note: past launches have low value; a future-launch list with UAE payloads would be useful (the window is past-only, despite the rolling 30-day wording in the project's readme).
- Launch-to-satellite match: the mission or launch name is scored against catalogue names (compact-text containment, shared tokens of 4 or more letters, generic words such as group, block, mission, launch, falcon and rocket ignored; threshold 12). Candidates whose international designator year differs from the launch year are rejected, so namesakes from other years never match. Search order: loaded core catalogue first, then the full active group fetched once per enable, with one delayed re-render (1.5 s) after it arrives.
- Approximate orbit when there is no match: altitude from the orbit name (550 km default low Earth, 20,200 km for "medium", 35,786 km for "geostationary" or "transfer"); direction due east (90 degrees) by default, south (180) for polar or sun-synchronous from northern pads and north (0) from southern pads, and 190 degrees for pads in western North America (latitude 20 to 60, longitude minus 140 to minus 105). The ring is a great circle through a point 12 degrees (8 for polar) downrange of the pad, 97 points. Real inclination, node and phase are not used, so it is a labelled illustration only.
- Failed or partially failed launches (status text matched) show "PLANNED . orbit" with no orbit, no replay and no live marker.
- Insertion time: the latest of deployment, separation, orbit insertion or injection events, else second-engine cutoff, else the last event; 600 s if no timeline. Estimated orbit period comes from Kepler's third law on the mean ring radius.
- Reconstructed ascent: a great-circle path from pad to insertion point; the fraction of horizontal travel follows the fourth power of time (near-vertical start), height follows a quarter sine, and an eastward shift from Earth rotation over an assumed 600 s climb is added; 512 intervals, with the last 52 points replaced by a cubic join matching the orbit's tangent, floored to the original altitude envelope so it cannot dip into the Earth. If upstream supplies stage trajectory points, per-stage polylines (6-colour cycle, 2 px) are joined to the orbit and only then may "replay" wording be used.
- Stage recovery endpoints, in order of trust: confirmed landing coordinates, return-to-launch-site (the pad), estimated from downrange distance along the ascent bearing (labelled as estimated downrange); separation placed 28 to 68 percent along the ascent.
- Replay timing: real ascent seconds come from the timeline insertion time, else path length divided by 9,000 m/s (at least 180 s), else 600 s; playback duration = 12 s times (real divided by 600), clamped 8 to 36 s; then one orbital lap in 28 s. The replay clock is wall time minus start, times speed; pause shifts the start so position and historical timestamp are preserved. The 5 s pre-countdown tile-settle wait is a separate phase shown as "preparing launch site", not part of the T-10 count. The camera is moved before the scene traversal to avoid a tile-refinement stutter loop. The launch-zone ring is a ground-classified primitive with a depth bias.
- Satellite dependency: the snapshot of prior parameters and enabled state is restored on exit; enabling fails closed if the satellite layer cannot start, and a restore failure raises an error.

### 8.1 Playback lag, mode inference and time provenance (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13d gap G6 and rows GND-35, GND-38, GND-41.
- Learned lag: the display clock runs behind real time by the 95th percentile of how late each feed's reports arrive (with the feed-wide 90th percentile considered) plus 5 s, clamped, floor 25 s, reset after 90 s of feed silence. A vehicle mid-segment finishes the segment at 1x even if the lag grows. Replacement of a quarantined outlier needs two coherent observations.
- Mode inference: a vehicle's mode comes from its route identifier using operator conventions (for example Boston: colour-named lines are rapid transit, an express-rail prefix is commuter rail, a boat prefix is ferry, numbers are buses; Norway: operator codespace; Helsinki: leading digits of the route code, a 10xx range for trams, an M suffix for metro, 1019 for ferry), falling back to the feed's default mode. A mode that is only the feed default is flagged inferred and gets the permissive 200 km/h ceiling, because a feed whose default is bus can also publish fast rail.
- Time provenance: every stored position records whether its timestamp came from the vehicle clock, the feed header clock or the time of receipt, so age text is honest.
- Housekeeping: off-screen vehicles skip animation (visibility sweep every 250 ms, rotation recompute every 200 ms); ground anchoring re-reads up to 6 times at 0.9 s; the card text refreshes 4 times a second; only Boston keeps server-side trail history (whether other feeds do is UNVERIFIED).

### 10.1 How the dots look like real traffic (census merge) [OBSERVED-IN-SOURCE]
Mechanism detail: 13d explainer A and gaps G1 to G5.
1. Road geometry comes from a map, not sensors: free vector tiles (OpenStreetMap-derived) plus, with a key, TomTom traffic tiles. Fetch centre is the look-at ground point, pulled toward nadir (never over 12 km away) when gazing at the horizon. Flow tiles are the relative style at zoom 12 (about 2.4 m quantisation), decoded in the browser into plain lon/lat lines; features without a numeric level are skipped unless closed (closed counts as level 0). Unusable data counts as free flow, never as a phantom jam. First dot paint waits at most 250 ms for flow data.
2. Flow-to-road snapping: flow lines and map roads share no identifiers, so flow is attached geometrically. Every flow line is chopped into short pieces filed into a grid of cells about 100 m wide by midpoint (long pieces subdivided so no cell is missed). Each map road is sampled at up to 7 evenly spaced points; each sample takes the nearest flow piece within 35 m whose compass direction is within 30 degrees of the road's direction of travel (a line flagged as both-way may match either direction; two-way map roads are modelled as two travel directions, each matched on its own). A road matches if at least half its samples (minimum 2) matched; its congestion value is the median of matched samples, so one bad piece cannot paint a whole road; it is closed if any matched piece is closed. Unmatched roads stay white simulated (or are hidden under the strict hide-uncovered policy, available only programmatically). Honesty rule: colour is real data, white is simulation.
3. Hybrid de-duplication: the TomTom line wins wherever it exists. A map road is tested by sampling it every 10 m against TomTom lines within 35 m, shrinking to 15 m when exactly one of the pair is a motorway or trunk mainline, so service roads 20 to 40 m beside a freeway survive. A partly covered road keeps only uncovered stretches of at least 40 m. Only drivable TomTom categories (motorway, international, major, secondary, connecting, major local, local, minor local) become vehicles or suppress map roads; unknown, private, parking and walking categories never do. TomTom classes map to the map classes used for speed, size and density (major behaves like primary, local like residential).
4. Dot count per road: floor of (length divided by spacing) times class density times user density scale times congestion factor times road weight, at least 1; spacing is 30 m under 1 km clearance, 80 m under 3 km, 150 m under 5 km, otherwise 250 m; two-way roads weigh 0.5 per direction. Direction: one-way roads follow the legal direction, two-way alternate by index.
5. Motion: start at a random place on a random segment; per-dot speed = class speed times user speed scale times a random 0.7 to 1.3 times the congestion factor (floor 0.15). Each road path is converted once to 3D points with precomputed segment lengths; each frame a dot advances by speed times elapsed time (elapsed capped at 0.1 s so a returning background tab does not teleport cars), carrying leftover metres across bends, positioned by straight-line interpolation with no per-frame trigonometry and objects reused. A dot reaching a road end shrinks away and re-enters at the start as a new vehicle with a new identity; births and retirements fade. In the first or last two segments each entry has a 0.8% chance of a 2 to 6 s pause, imitating a red light. Closed roads spawn no dots. A late flow match rescales speed and density in place without respawning.
6. Jam effects in the shipped default (density mode): a jam-bucket road gets platoons anchored at a random distance trailing 3 to 7 further cars at 6 to 12 m gaps (sizes 4 to 8, all sharing one direction, alternating by platoon on two-way roads), with stop-and-go (move 1.2 to 3 s at 2.2 times speed, stand 1.5 to 5 s) so the long-run average stays near the honest crawl speed; jam dots draw a pixel larger with a far-distance scale floor of 0.55. Heat lines (up to 400 corridors, pulsing about every 1.6 s through one shared material) are a separate default-off option; the choice is none, density, heat lines or both.
7. Ground placement: dots are lifted a few metres above the sampled surface (city mesh or terrain), eased in rather than popped, with unique height stations bounded per pass.
8. Sensor looks: dots are real 3D points that pass through full-screen look effects. Night vision and thermal reduce everything to brightness (a jam would read dimmer than free flow) and the retro look pixelates 4 to 6 px dots away. Treatment: mono looks (night vision, thermal, noir) give a bright white core with a thin dark halo, jam keeping a larger size; retro gives saturated primaries and a size boost; normal, anime and snow are untouched. Coloured live dots are floored at 5 px under any non-normal look; white simulated dots are never restyled; classification under these looks is carried by the detection bracket colour tier. A switch can force the normal palette; restyle happens in place without refetch.
9. Cost control: TomTom tiles pass through a server proxy so the key never reaches the browser; 2-minute cache (memory and disk), one fetch per tile at a time, a persistent daily request budget (default 6,000, sized to a 200,000-per-month free allowance) after which stale tiles or an explicit budget error are served.
